### Note

Speculative. A mostly standalone sign (16 of 24 tokens) whose syllabic value is undetermined: na leads ro and da by a hair (0.795, 0.769, 0.767), and NA (AB06) and \*301 already write na. Syllabic witnesses are few: \*86-SI-NI (HT140); \*86-RO, \*86 on face a and RO on face g of KHWa1013–1016, one scribe; RE-\*86 (MILZb4), the second sign incomplete. In \*86-SI-\*118-KA (HT140) \*86 lies in the break. The other tokens are standalone \*86 with a numeral or the \*86+\*188 ligature. HT11b gives no co-occurrence proof: there \*86 stands before the logogram KA and its numeral. Linear B \*86 survives at 6 attestations, at Knossos and Pylos, mostly in substrate toponyms; Melena proposes /dwa/ (Melena 1983, apud Salgarella 2023, 413–14). \*86 has a Phaistos Disk homomorph, PD 25, apart from NA's PD 23 (Duhoux 1983, 34 n. 2; Younger; Timm 2004, 207, apud Davis 2026b, 317–18 n. 802), and a Cretan Hieroglyphic one, CH 040 (CHIC 19, apud Davis 2026b, 342–43 n. 824). Davis finds the Disk and Cretan Hieroglyphic each encoding Linear A's language, or a very close relative, dialect or stage of it, so that Linear A values 'can tentatively be assigned' to their homomorphs (2026b, 339, 361, Hypotheses 35 and 39). Weak point: with \*86 = na, the Disk would write na with two signs, PD 25 and NA's PD 23.
