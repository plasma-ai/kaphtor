### Note

SY Za 9, Syme, HM 5585, found 1987 (Muhly and Olivier 2008, 212–13, photo and drawing). Olivier prints one group, 57-03-60-57-09; the division JA-PA-RA + JA-SE is di Mino's. JA-PA-RA yapra, 3 m. sg. ya- prefix of √p-r-y 'to bear fruit', both signs at their primary values; final -A the volitive yaqtula or the echo vowel. JA-SE yāṣē, as in SI-JA-SE (HT Zb 159, PE Zb 3): 'may what comes forth bear fruit', a prayer for the yield on a vessel of offering. Hebrew sets the two verbs in one verse, וְיָצָא חֹטֶר … וְנֵצֶר מִשָּׁרָשָׁיו יִפְרֶה 'a shoot shall come forth … and a branch from his roots shall bear fruit' (Isa 11:1), and calls the crop what comes forth from the field, הַיֹּצֵא הַשָּׂדֶה (Deut 14:22). Ugarit pairs pr 'fruit' with ybl 'produce' (KTU 1.5 II 5).

Rival: yapʾar(a) yāṣē 'may that which goes out glorify (her)', √p-ʾ-r with the ʾ unwritten as in JA-PA-QA yapqaḥ (PK Za 11, PK Za 12), the outpoured libation as subject. That verb belongs at the altar. 11Q5 XVIII 7–10 (Ps 154): ואדם מפאר עליון ירצה כמגיש מנחה … כמדשן מזבח 'a man who glorifies ʿElyon is accepted as one who brings a minḥa … as one who fattens the altar'. Ben Sira 50:20 (MS B), ובשם ייי התפאר 'and in the name of YYY he gloried', closes the high priest's rite after the wine poured at the foot of the altar (50:15). The deity as subject: Ps 149:4 יְפָאֵר, Sir 45:8 ויפארהו. Against it, √p-ʾ-r is Hebrew alone, in piʿel and hitpaʿel, which JA-PA-RA does not spell, and HALOT derives the verb from pĕʾēr 'headdress', a loanword from Egyptian pyr (908). A second rival: yabal yāṣē 'the yield that comes forth', Ug. ybl 'produce', with PA for b.

The reading is the editors' own, 57-03-60-57-09, on a circular serpentine libation table. Sign 3 is RA open to the right, as on SY Za 6 and SY Za 10 at the same sanctuary and in a fifth of Linear A; a DA with a low bar and a leaning stem they call 'not impossible' and list as SY Za 9\*. Sign 5 is incomplete but 'of the type' of the SE of SY Za 6, with its triangular base (Muhly and Olivier 2008, 213). They set the ending beside the -JA-SE of HT Zb 159 and PE Zb 3, find JA-PA a whole group on ZA 4a and call PA-RA a frequent pair (2008, 217): PA-RA stands in seven words of the corpus, PA-DA in three. Weak point: one attestation.
