### Note

2 standalone attestations across 2 sites (Malia, Phaistos). MAZb8: bare JA-SA on a clay vessel. PH16b: JA-SA on a fragmentary lame. IOZa12 and SAMWa1 are excluded as the libation formula: IOZa12 = JA-SA-SA-RA-ME whole on one line (Karetsou, Godart & Olivier, Kadmos 24, 1985, 143); SAMWa1 = JA-SA SA-RA (syllable sequence JA-SA-SA-RA = formula without terminal -ME, on a nodule with TE 10 ¹⁄₁₆). Clipped form without mimation (-amu); functions as independent verbal adjective or epithet in short votive inscriptions. Huehnergard: /yušru/ 'uprightness' (yuCCu pattern); confirms adverbial accusative with -m \|\| POLYSEMY HYPOTHESIS: LA sibilant merger (SA writes both /s/ and /š/) leaves standalone JA-SA ambiguous between √y-š-r 'righteous' and \*y-s 'to heal.' Full formula JA-SA-SA-RA-ME locks to √y-š-r (7 CONFIRMED sites). Standalone JA-SA at Malia (MAZb8) or fragmentary Phaistos (PH16b) could in principle preserve the healer reading, but neither site has specific Iasion cult associations. The Samothrace attestation (SAMWa1), which would have been the strongest anchor for the healer hypothesis, is now identified as the formula. Greek ἰάομαι 'to heal' has no secure IE etymology (Beekes: possible substrate); Iāsōn and Iasion both derive from it (Astour, Hellenosemitica 276–282). NWS -ān agentive suffix (Gordon §155) would produce \*yasā-ān &gt; Iāsōn, parallel to Rpan (√r-p-ʾ + -ān, Astour 252). LB i-ja-te (iatēr 'surgeon') at Pylos confirms the root in Mycenaean but not the PN. Cf. SI-RU-TE polysemy (√š-r-t / √š-y-r)—same sibilant merger, same root convergence
