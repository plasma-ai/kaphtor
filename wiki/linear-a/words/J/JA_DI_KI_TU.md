### Note

IO Za 2, position 2 of the libation formula. JA- is the vocative ya-, and DI-KI-TU spells Diqqitu, the geminate written single and the -TU vowel written, against Greek Δίκτη. The goddess named here is the addressee of the verb at position 1, A-TA-I-\*301-WA-JA ʾatainawaya 'I make myself a dwelling', the worshipper settling before her; each peak sanctuary invokes its mountain, Diqqitu at Iouktas and Palaikastro, Ida at Kophinas and Syme. Astour's etymology: Ug. dqt 'female head of small cattle for sacrifice' (Gordon UT §19.695), \*daqqatu, \*diqqatu, a divine name in the god-list KTU 1.102:8 (Astour 1966, 283–84); in the rituals Daqqītu receives a ewe after Pidray (KTU 1.39:15–16) and offerings beside Hebat in Hurrian rites (KTU 1.116:19; 1.132:7; Pardee 2000, 976), a goddess better known from Anatolia whose name Pardee takes as Semitic (2000, 79). Rival: 'the Little One', a goddess of Hebat's circle (DULAT 276). Hesiod calls the mountain Αἰγαίῳ ἐν ὄρει, 'Goat's Mountain' (Theog. 481–482 West), a Graeco-Semitic doublet, and Amenhotep III's list spells it Di-qa- with the determinative 'high' (Astour 1966, 'Aegean Place-Names'). Clay goats are the commonest votive at peak sanctuaries, and Amalthea, the goat who nursed Zeus in the Dictaean Cave, is the goddess brought down to a wet-nurse. Palaikastro, of Proto-Phoenician dialect, writes the nisbe -TE-TE, the vocative whole in JA-DI-KI-TE-TE-DU-PU₂-RE 'O Diqqitīt of the sanctuary' (PK Za 15) and JA-DI-KI-TE-TE-\*307-PU₂-RE, with the \*307 DUBUR logogram (PK Za 8), and weakened in A-DI-KI-TE-TE (PK Za 11, 12). See A-DI-KI-TE-TE.

### Note

IO Za 2, position 2 of the libation formula: vocative ya- before Diqqitu, the goddess of Dikte. Ugaritic dqt 'female of small cattle for sacrifice' is a divine name in a Ugaritic god-list (Astour 1966); Hesiod calls the mountain Aigaion oros, 'Goat's Mountain'. Palaikastro writes A-DI-KI-TE and JA-DI-KI-TE-TE (di Mino, Ya Diktu).
