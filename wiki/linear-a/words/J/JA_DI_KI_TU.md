### Note

Peak sanctuary libation formula position 2. Syllabary spells DI-KI-TU (/diktu/), not Greek Δίκτη (Dikte)—the -TU vowel is explicit. JA- = vocative particle ya-. The mountain-goddess is named at position 2 as the vocative addressee of the self-positioning verb at position 1 (ʾata-inawaya 'I make myself a dwelling')—the worshipper settles before the deity. Pattern: each peak sanctuary invokes a sacred mountain-deity (Diktu at Iouktas/Palaikastro, Ida at Kophinas/Syme) \|\| ASTOUR ETYMOLOGY: Ugaritic dqt 'female head of small cattle for sacrifice' (Gordon UT §19.695), pronounced \*daqqatu/\*diqqatu. dqt appears as a divine name in a Ugaritic god-list (Astour, 'New Evidence for the Semitic Derivation of the Greek Name of the Mountain Dikte,' 1966). Hesiod (Theog. 481–482 \[West\]) calls the same mountain Αἰγαίῳ ἐν ὄρει, Aigaion oros ('Goat's Mountain')—a Graeco-Semitic onomastic doublet. Egyptian scribal list of Amenhotep III spells it Di-qa- with determinative 'high' (Astour, 'Aegean Place Names,' 1966). Clay goat figurines are the most common votive offering at peak sanctuaries \|\| GREEK RECEPTION: Amalthea, the goat who nursed infant Zeus in the Dictaean Cave—demotion from sovereign deity to wet-nurse \|\| DIALECT: Variant A-DI-KI-TE(-TE) at Palaikastro shows the split: -TU (Iouktas, standard) vs -TE (Palaikastro, Proto-Phoenician). Vocative form JA-DI-KI-TE-TE attested on PKZa15 (JA-DI-KI-TE-TE-DU-PU₂-RE 'O Diktēt of the sanctuary') and PKZa8 (JA-DI-KI-TE-TE-\*307-PU₂-RE, same compound with \*307=DUBUR logogram)

### Note

IO Za 2, position 2 of the libation formula: vocative ya- before Diqqitu, the goddess of Dikte. Ugaritic dqt 'female of small cattle for sacrifice' is a divine name in a Ugaritic god-list (Astour 1966); Hesiod calls the mountain Aigaion oros, 'Goat's Mountain'. Palaikastro writes A-DI-KI-TE and JA-DI-KI-TE-TE (di Mino, Ya Diktu).
