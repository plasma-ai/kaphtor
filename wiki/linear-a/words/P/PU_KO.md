### Note

HT 31 .1 \[·\]-TI-SA · PU-KO · \*410-VS\[, a divider on each side. The five vessel names of the tablet are written into their signs, and \*402-VS 400 stands with no name; PU-KO stands apart, where a Haghia Triada heading has its transaction term, between the opening word and the first logogram. pūqū 'acquired, supplied', of √p-w-q: Ugaritic pq 'to obtain, acquire', Š 'to supply' (DULAT 666–667), Phoenician pwq 'to find, obtain' (DNWSI 903), Hebrew hēpîq (HALOT 920). Ugarit uses the verb of provisioning a feast with wine (KTU 1.4 VI 47). -KO writes final -qū, as -RO writes the final syllable of KU-RO.

Gordon, after Richard, read the heading as a command, mi-ti-sa pu-ko 'Go out from the depot!' (1976, 29–30, apud Duhoux 2002, 58 n. 49). Duhoux leaves open whether PU-KO belongs to the heading or qualifies the tripod cauldron, and prefers the second, the cauldron's material, 'en bronze' (Duhoux 2002, 35, 43–44). Rival: Qatna pūku (or būku, pūqu), an ornament of lapis lazuli or gold on gold vessels (CAD P 502). Weak point: the form is open between a passive plural and a verbal noun. One attestation. See PA-KU.
