### Note

PO-TO-KU-RO closes a tablet with the sum of its totals. On HT 122b, PO-TO-KU-RO 97 follows KU-RO 65 and counts both faces: HT 122a KU-RO 31 + KU-DA 1 + HT 122b KU-RO 65 = 97 (Schrijver 2014, 4). HT 131b ends two faces of figs, grain, oil, olives and wine in PO-TO-KU-RO 452½, and face a has the short form KU 353.

pūt kull 'the full extent of the total': pūt- 'front, edge' in construct before KU-RO kull, by the grammar a genitive, kulli, though the word is spelled as it stands alone. Akkadian pūtu is 'forehead, front' (CAD P 547), Hebrew pōt 'brow, forehead' (HALOT 983), Ugaritic pỉt 'temple' and 'boundary, border, edge' (DULAT 649). Akkadian builds the phrase on the sister word: (ana) pāṭ gimri 'entire extent', border with totality, GN ana pāṭ gimriša 'in its entirety' (CAD P 309), and Alalakh writes of a town pāṭašu gamram, 'its entire district' (CAD P 308). PO and TO write u, as RO does in KU-RO: o stands in 3% of Linear A syllabograms, and Davis takes it for secondary beside a, i and u, a hypothesis he calls 'tentative' (2026b, 159–61). Cuneiform writes pūtu SAG(.KI), and SAG is also rēšu and qaqqadu, the 'original amount' of an account (CAD R 288; CAD Q 109).

Weak point: Akkadian gives pūtu no accounting sense (CAD P 547–553). Rival: Gordon's bat kol 'daughter of all', po-to from \*butt- &lt; \*bunt-, the feminine of Ugaritic bun- 'son', which he suggests 'tentatively' (1966 §118). Two attestations. See KU-RO.
