### Note

PO-TO-KU-RO closes a tablet with the sum of its totals. On HT 122b, PO-TO-KU-RO 97 follows KU-RO 65 and counts both faces: HT 122a KU-RO 31 + KU-DA 1 + HT 122b KU-RO 65 = 97 (Schrijver 2014, 4). HT 131b ends two faces of figs, grain, oil, olives and wine in PO-TO-KU-RO 452½, and face a has the short form KU 353. The grand total exceeds that KU by 99½; both faces are broken at the top, so the sum cannot be checked, and no logogram follows it, the goods being of different kinds (Schoep 2002, 163). On HT 122 the sum takes in KU-DA 1, entered after KU-RO 31 (Schoep 2002, 163 n. 26); Gordon read the figure as 96 (1966 §118). Both faces of HT 122 are by HT Scribe 9.

pūt kull 'the full extent of the total': pūt- 'front, edge' in construct before KU-RO kull, by the grammar a genitive, kulli, though the word is spelled as it stands alone. Akkadian pūtu is 'forehead, front' (CAD P 547), Hebrew pōt 'brow, forehead' (HALOT 983), Ugaritic pỉt 'temple' and 'boundary, border, edge' (DULAT 649). Akkadian builds the phrase on the sister word: (ana) pāṭ gimri 'entire extent', border with totality, GN ana pāṭ gimriša 'in its entirety' (CAD P 309), and Alalakh writes of a town pāṭašu gamram, 'its entire district' (CAD P 308). PO and TO write u, as RO does in KU-RO: o stands in 3% of Linear A syllabograms, and Davis takes it for secondary beside a, i and u, a hypothesis he calls 'tentative' (2026b, 159–61). Cuneiform writes pūtu SAG(.KI), and SAG is also rēšu and qaqqadu, the 'original amount' of an account (CAD R 288; CAD Q 109). Hebrew pēʾâ 'side, edge, corner' is the same word, and Arabic has it as fiʾat 'crowd, battle troop' (HALOT 907–908).

The head or edge of a thing stands for the whole of it in Semitic counting. The census of Numbers opens śəʾû ʾet-rōʾš kol-ʿădat, 'lift the head of the whole congregation' (Num 1:2), and lərāʾšê haggəbārîm is 'by head-count', literally 'in respect of the total of the men' (1 Chr 24:4; HALOT s.v. rōʾš). Akkadian qaqqadu 'head' is the 'original amount, principal' of silver, barley and wine (CAD Q 109–110). Hebrew qāṣeh and qāṣâ 'end' give miqqāṣeh 'without exception' (Gen 19:4) and miqṣôt hāʿām 'from the whole population' (1 Kgs 12:31; HALOT 1120–1121). pūtu itself is the 'expanse of a country' (CAD P 549), and in Neo-Babylonian accounts it governs an amount: pūt šalšu, pūt 2-ta qātāti zitti 'a two-thirds share' (CAD P 553). Numbers closes its four camp totals as HT 122 closes its two faces: kol-pəqûdê hammaḥănōt 'all the counted of the camps', 603,550 (Num 2:32). The order is Semitic as well. Linear B extends to-so only by suffix, to-so-de and to-so-pa, and Linear A sets po-to- before ku-ro, a 'prefix' for Duhoux (2012, 12); a construct noun stands there by rule.

Kober singled out PO-TO-KU-RO from its context alone, before Linear B was read (Schoep 2002, 159). Schoep reads it as the 'grand total' of the KU-RO entries and adds that its tie to KU-RO 'may suggest' it carried the same implications for the transaction (2002, 163). Duhoux counts it, with KU-RO, among the two clearest words in the corpus (2012, 18). No cuneiform account uses pūtu to head a total; where it appears in accounts, it refers to shares (CAD P 547–553). Rival: Gordon's bat kol 'daughter of all', which he suggests 'tentatively' (1966 §118), taking po-to from \*butt- &lt; \*bunt-, the feminine of Ugaritic bun- 'son'. Two attestations. See KU-RO.
