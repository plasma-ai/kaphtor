### Note

A plain I-beam, a stem with a bar at top and bottom, alone before a number: HT 132 A-SE · \*319 5, the physician's entry, beside 27 ewes for the banquet; on the graffito HT Zd 156 it stands before each count of the series 1, 1½, 2¼, 3⅜, where Schrijver (2014, 14) and Corazza et al. (2021, 3) print \*319 four times and GORILA reads the first two as NE, each dotted (IV 133). The series is a sum growing by half each term, the interest joined to the capital (CAD Ṣ 159–160, s.v. ṣibtu A).

Silver is the good lent at interest, and five shekels of it are the physician's fee for setting a bone (Laws of Hammurabi § 221; CAD A/2 346, s.v. asû A), as HT 132 gives the physician 5. Copper in the ingot is excluded: the Linear B ingot signs are four-horned (Ventris and Chadwick 1956, 51, fig. 10), and none of the marked ingots of Haghia Triada carries the sign. The sign has no sound value, and no Linear A text spells the word.

Rival: a punctuation sign, GORILA's 'I p.-ê. ponctuation' for the graffiti (V 80), kept open by Salgarella for the two word-final tokens (2020, 332); on HT 132 a divider precedes the sign and 5 follows it, and GORILA indexes it there as a logogram (V 297). Rival: 'a probable transaction sign' on HT 132 (Steele 2024, 103). Rival: a measured unit length, from the sign's form (Stieglitz 1978, 129–130). Weak point: the reading rests on the one number 5, and nothing in the sign's form says silver. See A-SE and MA-TI-TI.
