### Note

MA 1, two faces of one bar, each whole: a I-DU-WI · \*47 · QE-DE-MI-NU ·, b A-MA · \*47 · QE-DE-MI-NU. A divider stands on each side of the sign, and the tabular copy sets it in a column of its own (GORILA I 268–269); the sign index classes the two as a monosyllabic word or transaction sign (V 213).

šū 'he of, that of', the determinative pronoun, with \*47 = so: Akk. šu 'he of', '1 GURUŠ šu PN one workman belonging to PN', 'x barley PN šu PN₂', and in names Šu-ilišu 'The-One-of-His-God' (CAD Š/3 152–153). Face b is 'the people of Qēdmīnu' and face a 'I-DU-WI, he of Qēdmīnu', the PN šu PN₂ of the Old Akkadian accounts. Its genitive is ši (CAD Š/3 152), the šī of SI-JA-SE, and \*47-NU-RA-JA opens on šū. SO writes šū as RO writes the -lu of KI-RO kilʾu. West Semitic keeps the pronoun: 'originally a determinative pronoun, weakened to a relative in Phoenician and Hebrew', with Phoenician ʾš, š and Hebrew šě-, and at Ugarit the name šbʿl 'the one of Baal' (HALOT 1365); Phoenician šl- is 'of' (Krahmalkov 461). Ugaritic has the frame with its own pronoun and this root, ʿr d qdm 'the city of the East' (KTU 1.100:62; Tropper 2000, 237), and Ebla names open with dū- and with šu- (DULAT 252).

Rival: ḏū, with \*47 = do, Ug. d 'the one(s) of' (DULAT 252), syllabic du-u (Tropper 2000, 134); the lexicon writes ḏū with DU. Rival: after the pronoun QE-DE-MI-NU is 'the Easterners', Heb. qadmōnî (Gen 15:19), and face b 'the people of the East'. Weak point: one bar, and \*47's value stands on \*47-KU-NA alone. See DU, SI-JA-SE, QE-DE-MI-NU, A-MA and \*47-NU-RA-JA.
