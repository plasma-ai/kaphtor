### Note

Alone on seven Khania roundels, KH Wc 2059, 2091 and 2092 being fragments of one (RILA-S1), and after VIR+\[?\] on KH Wc 2117. HT 8b lists SU-PU₂-\*188 1 and PA₃-\*188 2 then has \*86+\*188 ½; HT 26b has \*188-\*86 7. GORILA's drawings set the gridded \*188 on a long hull with a raised end (KH Wc 2058, 2059, 2062, 2117). Uchitel reads \*188 'depicted above the ship' as 'a schematic drawing of a ship-load' (2016b, 9), and Hallager calls A 566 'the so-called ship-ideogram' (1995, 93). The ½ measures the terebinth, so the ship marks how it came: terebinth by sea, as the Uluburun ship carried 'a ton of terebinth resin' (Cline 2014, 77).

Rival: Schoep takes \*86 for the main component and \*188 for its qualifier, so that the ligature may 'designate a group of people' (2002, 136); men are counted whole, and HT 8b has ½. Second rival: a ship-load of any goods, Uchitel's \*188. In GORILA's drawings KH Wc 2061, 2097 and 2109 lack the hull, and KH Wc 2060 keeps the hull alone. See \*86, \*188, \*337+\*188.
