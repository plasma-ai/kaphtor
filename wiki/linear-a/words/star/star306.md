### Note

A logogram at Khania, alone before whole numbers on tablets of mixed commodities: KH 4 and KH 11 \*306 4, each under A-DU-RE-ZA and before CYP ⅓; KH 11 \*306 1; KH 6 AU-RE-TE · \*306 1; broken on KH 47, KH 84 and KH 87; GORILA prints \]\*306 100 on KH 21. It stands ahead of CYP, where otherwise only VIR stands (Schoep 2002, 125), and is joined to VIR on KH 19, \*306-VIR+\*307 13, the servants who are men of the sanctuary, and to CYP on KH 2. On HT 94a \*318-\*306 11 sets it after the table sign, where HT 45 has \*305, a class of persons: the servants of the table. Uchitel reads numbered persons recorded against commodities and the sign as 'woman', 'a very schematic picture of a human being' (Uchitel 2003, 83–84). \*306 at its proposed value ʿa: ʿabdu 'servants', the class Ugarit lists as ʿbdm and feeds with ḥpr ʿbdm 'rations of the servants' (KTU 4.636:3; DULAT 136–37). Cuneiform writes the word with one sign, ÌR = ar-du = ab-du in the Ugarit polyglot (Ug 5 137 III 4; DULAT 136), and Linear B shortens ko-wo 'boy' and ko-wa 'girl' to KO in the Knossos personnel series (Bartoněk 2003, 123). Rival: a head of livestock, tentatively the cow (Schoep 2002, 125), then ʿiglu 'calf' (Ug. ʿgl, DULAT 149–50), which the single unit on KH 6 favours, AU-RE-TE · \*306 1 where other names have a he-goat, an ox or \*334, with \*23M on the line below it on the broken KH 87; a servant made over to AU-RE-TE fills the place as well; a lone ʿ on a Palmyrene tessera is taken for ʿglʾ 'the calf' (Seyrig apud DNWSI 824). Rival: the ewe; the editors of KH 100 take \*306 for 'probably the same ideogram as OVISf in Linear B' (Andreadaki-Vlasaki and Hallager 2007, 12), and Khania writes no \*21F as a logogram (GORILA V 176); the ewe and the she-goat have their own signs, \*21F and \*22F. Weak points: no witness spells the word, and A-PA₃-DI writes the root with A. Khania draws a loop beside the junction and two level strokes at upper right, where Haghia Triada hangs the loop below two steep strokes; KH 100 writes \*352-\*306 with the Khania form, so the forms differ by site and not by use; Salgarella has the Haghia Triada sign 'drawn in a slightly different manner' from Khania's, the more realistic of the two (2020, 164). See \*318, VIR+\*307, I.
