### Note

HT 10b .2 \*312-TA 6½, whole and undotted, after TA-RI-NA 15 and before KA-SA-RU 'Fuller' 6, in the list that continues the emmer of side a (GORILA I 21), where DA-RE 'the estate' and U-\*325-ZA 'the storehouse' draw too. Zakros spells it \*312-TA₂: ZA 6b \*312-TA₂ GRA+PA 40. Davis lists the two among sequences attested in slightly different forms in more than one region (2026b, 77 and n. 284). With \*312 = BI, bīta 'the house': Ug. bt (DULAT 243–247), Heb. bayit (HALOT 124); the household draws grain beside the estate. Ugarit heads a register of spelt and wheat spr ḥpr bt, ḥpr bt 'ration(s) of the temple / palace' (KTU 4.269:1), books goods l bt 'for the palace' (KTU 4.868:3; DULAT 245), and writes the word bi-tu in its syllabic lists (Ug 5 137; DULAT 243). Ugaritic writes house, daughter and cloth length alike as bt, and the sign alone is bitt, the length of cloth, on KH 8, ARKH 3a and HT 123+124b and inside the cloth sign on HT 38. Weak point: PI-TA-KA-SE Bīt-kissē writes the same word with PI, and PA-JA-TA writes it bayta. Egypt writes the Canaanite word both ways, ba₂-ya-ta with the diphthong and the commoner bi-ta contracted (Hoch 1994, nos. 113, 144), and Emar writes bi₄-tá (GlOS 1, 135): PA-JA-TA is the first, PI-TA- and \*312-TA the second.

Rival: bitta 'lengths of cloth', the clothing allotment, the word of the lone sign. Second rival: a man's name with \*312 = ZO, Melena's "zo?-ta?" beside the zo-ta of the Knossos tablets (2014, 46 n. 50). On KN L 588.2 zo-ta-qe stands over the cloth sign, "almost universally taken" as a man's name, though "we cannot finally exclude" the name or description of a cloth (Killen 2024, 870).
