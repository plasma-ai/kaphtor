### Note

Six Haghia Triada attestations. First word on HT 102 (SA-RA₂ GRA 976 follows; KU-RO 1060), HT 94a (VIR+\[?\] 62 follows on the line; KU-RO 110), HT 6a (divider, then DA-TA-RA) and HT 105 (broken after PA; VIR+\[?\] 234 below). An entry with ½ on HT 8b and HT 140; KA-PA-QE 5¾ on HT 6a.

Hebrew and Aramaic kĕpar 'village', with final /r/ unwritten; Bronze Age kapr 'village' at Ebla, Alalakh VII and on the Euphrates, Ug. kpr (GlOS 1, 315). Linear B keeps a place ka-pa (KN E 71) and its ethnic ka-pa-jo (KN B 5752), among men levied by place (Uchitel 2016a, 18).

KA-PA, with SA-RA₂ and I-KA, goes with grain and shipment, DA-QE-RA with olive oil, and Uchitel places KA-PA in the watered Timbaki valley (Uchitel 2016a, 25). On HT 140 KA-PA ½ stands against the oil as KA 1 and \]KA 1 do: there a vessel, kappar, the geminate written single, Emar 1 DUG kap-pár-ra, Heb. kĕpôr 'small metal bowl', Akk. kapparnu 'pitcher' (GlOS 1, 315), with KA its abbreviation (Montecchi 2010, 15). On HT 8b the ½ stands among recipients. Rival: Akkadian gabbu 'all' (Gordon, via Rendsburg 1996, 38), spelt ka-pa-šu-nu and qa-pa-šu-ma at Nuzi, where it follows a total of men (Gordon 1934, 20 §100). It fits the heads of HT 94a and HT 105, but not the ½ entries, and HT 94a closes with KU-RO. Uchitel's own etymology is Anatolian hapa/i- 'river' (Uchitel 2016a, 25). See KU-PA-RI.
