### Note

KI-RO books what is still owed, set against KU-RO 'total' and never counted into it. HT 34.6 balances: SA+MU+KU 100, then PA₃ 70 and KI-RO 30, 70 handed over and 30 outstanding (Schrijver 2014, 7). HT 123a gives each olive entry its oil (\*308) and a small KI-RO, totals the olives at KU-RO 93½, their exact sum, and the oil at just over 25, then sets KI-RO 6 after both. HT 15 books 684 and 570, then KI-RO 400, and HT 30 re-lists its commodities under KI-RO in smaller quantities. At the head of a roster, KI-RO opens names at 1 apiece that close in KU-RO (HT 88, 6; HT 117a, 10; HT 94b, 5), and a total cannot head a list. KI-RO never shares a tablet with TE; on HT 30 and HT 34 it heads a section beside SA-RA₂ (Schoep 2002, 164). TE books what was paid in, SA-RA₂ what is due, KI-RO the part of it withheld.

kilʾu 'what is held back', the qitl noun of √k-l-ʾ 'hold back, withhold': RO for l, the glottal stop unwritten, -u the case ending. Hebrew keeps the base in keleʾ 'imprisonment', kilʾô with the suffix (2 Kgs 25:29), and Egyptian group writing spells the Canaanite noun ka-ru₂-ʾu /kalʾu/ 'prison', r for l as KI-RO writes it (Hoch 328). In the ledgers the verb names what was kept back. Akkadian kalû is 'to withhold goods, merchandise, deliveries' (CAD K 99): 'last year you withheld the rest of the barley of my provision' (YOS 3 40:15). Aramaic has \[p\]rskn zy kly 'your salary which has been withheld' (DNWSI 508), and Arabic kāliʾ is 'a debt of which the payment is deferred' (Lane 2624). KU-RO kull- (√k-l-l) differs in vowel and third radical.

Peruzzi first gave KI-RO the sense of a shortfall. Killen objected that some contexts do not bear it out and that HT 123's KI-RO entries keep regular proportions (Schoep 2002, 163). With KI-RO added, HT 123's olives stand close to 4 to 1 with their oil (Uchitel 2016b, 14): KI-RO is the balance of a fixed quota, so the proportions are regular. Schoep reads 'deficit', clearest where KI-RO follows KU-RO with a smaller entry (HT 123a.9; less clearly HT 93b.1), and finds that most occurrences suit it (Schoep 2002, 163–164). Fifteen occurrences on ten tablets, all at Haghia Triada. See KU-RO.
