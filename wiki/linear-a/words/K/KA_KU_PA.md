### Note

HT 16 KA-KU-PA ·, the heading over DI-NA-U ⅛, WA ⅓, \*188+KU ⅜ and SA-PO ZE 1⁄16; on the roundels HT Wc 3015 KA-KU-PA with CYP and HT Wc 3016 with \*304+PA and CYP+D. kakkub 'flask': Akk. kukkubu, a small container of metal, glass or clay serving as alabastron, libation jar and drinking flask (CAD K 499). The Amarna gift lists fill it with perfume, and Alašia is asked for two, 'send me two k.'s of perfume' (EA 35:25; CAD K 499); Ras Shamra has bronze vessels 'with their flagons', ku-ku-bi-ši-na (RS 16.146+:32). The flask heads what goes into it: cyperus, an aromatic, and fat on the roundels, small fractions of terebinth (\*188) and three other items on HT 16.

Ugarit has the name kkbn, 'shaped like a k.-vessel' (Watson 1993, 216), 'flask(-shaped)' after Xella (Watson 1995, 223). KA writes ka where Akkadian has ku. Rival: kawkab 'Star', a place name, as Mari's town Kabkab (MTT I/1, s.v. Kabkab): Hebrew kôkāb (HALOT 463), Ugaritic kkbm 'stars', assimilated from kbkbm (KTU 1.10 I 4; DULAT 422–23), Punic kkb 'star' (DULAT 422), KKB, Kakkabe, a Sidonian colony (Krahmalkov 2000, 225), and the name kkb (KTU 4.767:1). Also compared: Hitt. kakkapa- (Watson 1996b, 100); the Linear B name ka-ka-po (West, in DULAT 429). Three attestations, all Haghia Triada. See PA-KU.
