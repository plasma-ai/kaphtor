### Note

KU-RO closes a list with the sum of its entries. HT 89 is the control: 23+22+24+13+5, KU-RO 87. So HT 85a (12+12+6+24+5+3+4, KU-RO 66), HT 11b (40+30+50+30+30, KU-RO 180), HT 104 (45½+20½+29, KU-RO 95), HT 9b (24) and HT 25b (28+24, KU-RO 52), and the rosters at 1 apiece: HT 88, 6; HT 117a, 10; HT 94b, 5. HT 127b totals twice: KU-RO \*307 156 is one section, and with KU 72, 24, 15, 11 and the erased 14 it closes at KU-RO 292. On HT 122 the two faces' KU-RO 31 and 65, with KU-DA 1, make PO-TO-KU-RO 97. Before a logogram KU-RO totals by commodity: HT 123a KU-RO OLIV 93½, the exact sum of the olives; HT 116b KU-RO GRA 100\[, \*304 15, OLE 17, where the recto's \*304 is 12+3; ZA 15b KU-RO VIN 78; PH(?) 31a KU-RO over the livestock. The scribe is one off on HT 94a (111 for 110) and HT 119 (159 for 160) (Schoep 2002, 160 n. 242). KI-RO 'owed' stands against the total and outside it (HT 123a, HT 123b).

kull 'all, the whole': RO writes l, and the doubled consonant is written once (Gordon 1966 §§143, 145). Hebrew closes its lists with the word. Joshua 12 counts the conquered kings 'one' apiece and ends kol-məlākîm šəlōšîm wəʾeḥād 'all the kings, thirty-one' (Josh 12:24), as HT 88 ends six names at 1 in KU-RO 6 (Gordon 1966 §117; Rendsburg 1996, 42); so kōl šəlōšîm wəšibʿāh 'in all, thirty-seven' (2 Sam 23:39) and hakkōl 'the total' (Ezra 2:42). Ugaritic kl /kull-/ 'totality' (Tropper 2000, 244) counts the same way: l klhm 'according to the total (number)' (KTU 1.43:26), and wine 'assigned in its entirety', klh (KTU 4.230:1; DULAT 432–433). Old Assyrian keeps the u, nominative kulu beside kalu (CAD K 87), and Akkadian kullatu is 'all, totality' (CAD K 504). KU alone is its short form, KU-RA its -a form, PO-TO-KU-RO its compound. KI-RO kilʾu (√k-l-ʾ) differs in vowel and third radical.

Kober set KU-RO, PO-TO-KU-RO and KI-RO apart by context before Linear B was read (Schoep 2002, 159). For Schoep the position leaves no doubt of 'total', though KU-RO need not match Linear B to-so, which never stands opposite o-pe-ro as KU-RO stands opposite KI-RO (Schoep 2002, 160–162). Gordon read kull- from HT 88 (1966 §117), and Rendsburg reads ku-ro /kull-/ 'all' (Rendsburg 2026, 203 n. 4). For Duhoux KU-RO and PO-TO-KU-RO are the two clearest words of the corpus, and the Lycian reading cannot account for them (Duhoux 2012, 18). Rival: a loanword. Davis holds the meaning secure whatever the language, and the Semitic loan the most widely accepted etymology, its source West Semitic: Arabic kull, Hebrew kol and Geʿez kʷəllu against Akkadian kalû (Davis 2026b, 8 and n. 28). Thirty-seven occurrences on 34 tablet faces, at Haghia Triada, Zakros and Phaistos. See KU, KU-RA, PO-TO-KU-RO, KI-RO.
