### Note

London Medical Papyrus (BM 10059), Spell 33, the last word, after 'my Healer, the Great One': ʾ-m-ʾa with the god classifier, the reed-leaf and man with hand to mouth writing ʾ (Steiner 1992, n. 15), mꜥ the m, the reed-leaf and vulture ʾa (Hoch 1994, 12–13). ʾimmā 'Mother', \*ʾu/imm-: Ug. um (DULAT 67–68), Heb. ʾēm (HALOT 61), with the Aramaic emphatic -ā. Spell 27, against the same ḥ-m-k-tu, names 'our Lord' and then ʾ-s-t-ʾu-m, Ištar-ummī 'my mother Ištar' (Steiner 1992, 193 and n. 20). Weak points: Spell 27 writes 'mother' ʾu-m, with u; the emphatic ending is early for the 14th century. Rivals: Haider's Ameja, set beside Linear B a-me-ja (Haider 2001, 480); Mayyā 'Water' and Akulov's Maja, which drop the first group. See RP-33-Y and P3-WR.
