### Note

GORILA's A 550, on seven tablets at two sites (HT 4, KH 3): the core MI+JA with RU set over it. The oil recipes book it part for part with the oils, ≈⅙ beside four oils on HT 23a, ⅓ beside OLE+RI and \*308 on HT 35, ½ beside \*308 on KH 12: a liquid. Cloth stands beside it twice, both times on a broken line: KH 60 books it ⅕ under \]RA-KI-TA-NA-SI\[, the linen heading, and PH 3b has \]MI+JA ¾ on the back of the wool list PH 3a. HT 60 has the core with its third sign lost, MI+JA+\[ \] ¹⁄₁₆, between OLE+RI and VIN. HT 81 books it to the A-SE, and on HT 34.5 Younger and SigLA read this sign at 245 where GORILA draws MI+JA+I.

Read largest sign first it spells mi-ja-ru, and Salgarella sets it beside Linear B mi-ja-ro, KN Ln 1568.1, a correspondence she calls "however speculative", with the man's name mi-ja-ra-ro as the alternative (2022, fig. 15). mi-ja-ro is the dyeing: it heads that list, where each woman's piece is booked mi TELA+TE or pe TELA+TE, and Phaistos has mi TELA+TE 14 against pe TELA+TE 2 on KN Le 641. The list keeps three kinds apart, plain TELA+TE beside pe and mi on one line (Ln 1568.2), and its edge sends work o-pi po-ni-ke-ja, to the house of Phoinikeia, a woman named from φοῖνιξ (Killen 2024, s.vv. o-pi, po-ni-ke-ja). TE is te-pa, the cloth booked as wa-na-ka-te-ra, the king's, on KN Lc 525, and pe is pe-ko-to 'combed'; Duhoux finds 'stained, defiled' strange in a manufacturing context, "perhaps better … 'rough' vel sim." (in Killen 2024, 727), while Luján takes it as μιαρός, with mi its abbreviation: 'in Mycenaean, however, it may mean that the cloth has been dyed' (2010, 380–381). Greek keeps the word in μιαίνω, of a woman staining ivory with crimson (Il. 4.141), the passage that shows Blanc 'que le sens premier doit être « imprégner, teindre », sans nuance péjorative, sans nuance de souillure' (2001, 156–157), and in μιαρός, 'rather Pre-Greek' for Beekes, who has "Myc. mi-ja-ro, probably of colored fabrics" (EDG 950–951): the dye, and cloth dyed with it.

The raw material of murex purple is a liquid from the gland of the shellfish, simmered in vats, and Crete has its earliest remains (Stieglitz 1994, 46–54); at Knossos po-ni-ki-jo, paid from da-wo near Phaistos, is on Foster's suggestion a red dyestuff that coloured perfumed oil and dyed cloth, though its identity "remains uncertain" (Killen 2024, 549). The women of Phaistos make the mi cloth on Le 641, with those of do-ti-ja and the te-pe-ja of Knossos (Luján 2010, 379), and da-wo has seven on Ln 1568.10; Le 5930 has at least twelve (Nosch 2024, 333 n. 64).

miyāru 'the changing', the qitāl verbal noun of √m-w-r 'to change, exchange' (Heb. hēmîr, tĕmûrâ; HALOT 560, 1747), y for w after i as in Arabic qiyām from √q-w-m. Akkadian names dyed wool by a verbal noun in the same trade: ṣirpu 'red dyed wool', from ṣarāpu 'to dye red', sent between courts (EA 12:15) and issued as work assignments (CAD Ṣ 208).

The dye is the thing that changes: in the vat it turns dusky, black and air-coloured and only then sea-purple (Ps.-Aristotle, De coloribus 797a, 795b), true tekhelet is known by how its colour changes under test (b. Menaḥot 43a), and Hebrew uses this root of a substance whose quality turns, rêḥô lōʾ nāmār 'its scent did not change' (Jer 48:11; HALOT 560). Arabic uses it of a liquid in motion: māra d-damu 'the blood ran, flowed upon the ground', said of tears as well (Lane 2743).

M. G. Nouira, who makes murex purple by hand at Carthage, keeps more than a hundred extracts 'indexed by blend and light exposure', light shifting 'the raw secretion through a spectrum of blues and violets before it sets as true imperial purple': 'As many shades of purple as there are stars hanging from the heavens.' Exposed to light before it fully oxidizes, the same secretion 'settles as blue (indigotin) rather than the deep reddish-purple produced by full oxidation' (argamanpurple.com, The Craftsman; Tekhelet).

Weak points: no Semitic text uses √m-w-r of dye, and Salgarella doubts that the core is MI+JA (2020, 145–146). Rivals: mayya 'liquid' as the core, MI-JA 'water' on SY Za 5 and Akk. mû 'fluid, liquid matter' of juices and infusions (CAD M/2 154–155), with RU a rider naming the kind as I and KA do, which leaves Linear B mi-ja-ro as the ligature spelled out; Linear B mi-ja-ro as uncombed woollen cloth of rough appearance (Firth 2012, 232, in Pierini 2022, 48 n. 17), the same cloth as plain TELA+TE for Killen (1987, ibid.), a sense μιαρός has nowhere in Greek, drawn from its standing against pe, where Homer's μιαίνω is already a craft word, of ivory stained with φοῖνιξ, Ln 1568 books plain TELA+TE apart from mi, a kind Nosch counts with pe-ko-to among 'the more rare types' (2024, 333), the tablet's abbreviations marking 'types and perhaps colours of TELA+TE' (2012, 327), and the Linear A commodity is a measured liquid. See MI+JA+I, MI+JA+KA, MA-RI-RE-I.
