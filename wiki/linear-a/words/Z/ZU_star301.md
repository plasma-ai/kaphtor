### Note

ARKH 2 .3–4 ZU-\*301-SE-DE-\*21F-\*118 6, read ṯannān ṣēd ḥiṭṭa 'the chariot soldiers: provision of wheat, 6', under SI-DA-TE ṣēdat 'provisions', between A-SI-DA-TO-I 12 and A-SU-PU-WA 4 (GORILA III 6–7). \*301 and \*21F over erasures, no dividers. SE-DE ṣēd, √ṣ-y-d as SI-DA-TE.

Ugarit lists the ṯnnm as a group with named members (KTU 4.68:70; 4.126:4), with a rb ṯnnm (4.382:5), and in Kirta the singular ṯnn names the whole troop (KTU 1.14 II 38; DULAT 909): ZU-\*301 carries no plural ending either. A syllabic list of professions writes LÚ ša-na-nu-ma (PRU 6 93:6), which Huehnergard normalises /ṯannānu/ (2008, 187). Hoch reconstructs the same form behind Egyptian snny, 'probably the "professional" qattālu' (1994, 261–262, no. 371). ZU writes ṯ before any vowel or none, as in A-ZU-RA ʾAṯirat.

Egyptian snny, 'of the chariotry', takes the arrow determinative (Hoch 1994, 262). Alalakh counts 1006 šanannu, names them beside charioteers and allots sheep to them as a body (CAD Š/1 366). Hebrew šinʾān stands beside the chariots of Elohim (Ps 68:18), ʾalpê šinʾān 'thousands of warriors' on Albright's reading, a derivation from Ug. ṯnn that HALOT calls 'the least probable' of four (1596–1597).

Weak point: DULAT sets a query after 'archer' and records Vita's rejection of that sense (909); the must list turns to wheat without a logogram; \*21F-\*118 takes its numeral without one on ZA 14 too (\*21F-\*118 1). Rival: one name ZU-\*301-SE-DE + ḥiṭṭa 6. One witness.
