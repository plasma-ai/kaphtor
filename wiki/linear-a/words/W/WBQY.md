### Note

London Medical Papyrus (BM 10059), Spell 33: w-\[b\]-q with the disease classifier, the first group of the spell, the b damaged. Steiner transcribes w-b-ḳi and leaves it unread (Steiner 1992, 198); Kyriakidis reads the last sign as l, w-b-ql, and finds that 'the combination wbq may suggest a Semitic language' (Kyriakidis 2002, 213–14). Egyptian q writes q, g and ġ (Hoch 1994, 411–13), and only q gives a disease: baqq- 'rot', \*bVḳ-, Mandaic baq and baqiata 'decay, gangrene', after w-. The disease is addressed, and S3T and S3-BW command it. Rivals: baqq- 'gnat', Akk. baqqu, as sāmānu is also 'a noxious insect' (CAD S 111); Akk. epqu 'leprosy' (CAD E 246), with w for its first vowel; Heb. bōhaq (Lev 13:39; HALOT 112), with the h unwritten; Arabic √w-b-q 'perish' (Q 18:52; 42:34). Weak points: these spells write q with k (Steiner 1992, at n. 67); a conjunction opens the spell.
