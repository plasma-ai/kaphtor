### Note

Egyptian group-writing: wbqy \[rubric element 4, Akulov 2023, 3\], disease determinative; Kyriakidis reads final sign as l (w-b-ql), Steiner w-b-qi(?). Read as wa- 'and' + \*b(u)haq-ī: SED #135 \*bahaḳ- '(kind of) skin disease'—Heb bōhaq 'tetter' (Lev 13:39), JAram bōhăqā, Syr behqītā 'scabies', Mandaic baqiata 'decay, gangrene' (h-less, precedent for unwritten h here), Arab bahaq (still clinical for vitiligo), Mehri/Harsusi/Jibbali white-patch terms, Tigre bähaq 'blisters'. Names a skin disease beside samāwnā (Akk sāmānu 'the red one') in a skin-disease papyrus. Rivals: Arabic-only √w-b-q 'perish' (Q 18:52, 42:34—'the Perdition'); Akulov's Eg. wbnw 'wound' (n→q unexplained). Kyriakidis 2002, 215: 'the combination wbq may suggest a Semitic language'. Steiner left unread. Weak points: unwritten medial h; the final sign is disputed.
