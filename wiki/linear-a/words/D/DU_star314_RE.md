### Note

KO Za 1 .2 TU-RU-SA · DU-\*314-RE · I-DA-A ·, after A-TA-I-\*301-WA-JA; GORILA IV 20 dots DU and RE, and \*314 is whole, three wavy strokes as at Phaistos. ḏū har ʾIda 'the One of Mount Ida', the title of Tursa: DU the relative 'that of' (see DU) and har 'mountain' in the construct, Heb. har (HALOT 254), Ug. hr (DULAT 341), the Amarna gloss ḫa-ar-ri; final RE writes the bare r, as in DU-RA-RE. Titles of this build: Ugaritic d 'the one(s) of' (DULAT 252), the Sabaic gods ḏ-S¹mwy (CIH 519) and ḏt-Ḥmym (CIH 495), and Dushara, god of al-Sarāh (Faris 1952). \*314 writes the h as the guttural signs do, which do not tell h from ḥ and ḫ: \*21F writes ḫatun, ḥiṭṭa and Unuḫu (see \*21F-TU-NE, \*21F-\*118, U-NU-\*21F), and cuneiform writes this word with its ḫ sign, ḫa-ar-ri (EA 74:20).

Rival: Facchetti (1999, 132) and Davis (2013, 44 n. 46) take \*314 here as an orthographic variant of PU₂, and Thomas (2020, 8) admits the comparison: DU-PU₂-RE dubur 'sanctuary' (PK Za 15, HT Zb 160), dubur ʾIda 'the sanctuary of Ida', a value for this word alone, in a sign unlike PU₂. One attestation. See A-RI, DU-PU₂-RE.
