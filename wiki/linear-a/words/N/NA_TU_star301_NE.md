### Note

Skoteino Cave inked inscription (fragmentary—lacuna on both sides). With \*301=NWY logogram (CONFIRMED, phonetic /na/), full reading is NA-TU-NA-NE \|\| DUAL ANALYSIS: (A) PASSIVE—natūn (qatūl passive participle of √n-t-n 'to give') + -ān = natūnān 'the dedicated one.' TU vowel matches /ū/ of the passive participle exactly; dead vowel -e on final NE closes the syllable: na-tū-nā-n(e). BIBLICAL PARALLEL: Hebrew nĕtūnīm (Num 3:9) 'given ones'—Levites as persons dedicated to sanctuary service. Post-exilic Nĕtīnīm (Ezra 2:43, 8:20; 1 Chr 9:2) are temple servants from √n-t-n; the institution is pre-Israelite, traced to the Gibeonites (Josh 9:27). (B) ACTIVE—nātān (qātāl active participle) + -ān = nātānān 'the giver.' Parallels A-TA-NA = ʾatanā &lt; \*yātanat 'She-who-gives' (proposed NWS etymology of Athena, √y-t-n). If active, natūnān qaqqatī = 'The Giver at the Peak'—theophanic title for the Goddess at a cave sanctuary. A-TA-NA/NA-TU-NA paradigm would attest both feminine divine epithet and masculine/neuter cultic title from the same root \|\| Both readings contextually strong: sanctuary dedicatee (passive) or divine epithet (active) at a sacred cave. \*301=NWY is CONFIRMED across 288 occurrences at 16 sites. \|\| Huehnergard: Ug ntn, ttn /tatinū/ "to set, put; to give"; I-n verb; preterite yaqtul 3mp: ttn /tatinū/. Assimilation of /n/ standard for I-n verbs. BH cognate: nātan "to give" (Huehnergard 2012, p. 52 (paradigm); pp. 6-7 (Appendix C))
