### Note

PL Zf 1, a silver pin: … TA-NU-NI-KI-NA · NI-NU-NI · \[?\]-I-\[?\]. NI-NU-NI follows TA-NU-NI-KI-NA tanūḥūn qīna 'they wail a dirge' as its subject, on a funerary pin: nīnūn 'offspring, descendants', Heb. nîn 'descendants', with the verb nyn 'to get descendants' (Ps 72:17; HALOT 696). NI writes nī, NU nū, and the final NI the n with a dead vowel. Weak points: Hebrew has nîn only as a collective singular, beside neked (HALOT 696), with no cognate at Ugarit, where offspring is špḥ; and the masculine plural is written -NA elsewhere (DI-RI-NA, TU-\*21F-RI-NA), so -NU-NI is no regular plural. Rivals: nīn-un, with nominative nunation as in Arabic; a personal name, as Ugaritic nn, ni-in₄-na (DULAT 624; Kinlaw 1967, 87). One attestation.
