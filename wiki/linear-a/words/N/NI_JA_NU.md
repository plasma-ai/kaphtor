### Note

KN Zc 6 .2, a word of its own between two dividers, after QI-TI-RI 'my incense', A-DI DA-KI-TI 'unto Daqqitu' and PA-KU 'flask' (GORILA IV 121; NI is dotted in one of its two transcriptions). niyāḥ-nu 'our soothing (offering)': the incense named for what it does before the goddess, as Hebrew rêaḥ nîḥōaḥ 'soothing odour' is the fixed term of the burnt offerings (Gen 8:21; Exod 29:41; Lev 1:13; HALOT 1226–1227) and Aramaic nîḥôḥîn are offered to the God of heaven (Ezra 6:10).

The root is n-w-ḥ 'rest, be calm': Hebrew nwḥ (HALOT 679), nwḥ 'to be calm, at rest' in Old Canaanite, Phoenician, Hebrew and Aramaic (DNWSI 721), Ugaritic /n-ḫ/ 'to rest' and mnḫ 'calm' (DULAT 620–621, 556), Amarna nu-uḫ-ti (EA 147:56), Akk. nâḫu (CAD N/1 143). The noun with y is Aramaic nyḥʾ 'rest' (Targum Jonathan, 1 Kgs 8:56). NI-JA writes ni-yā; the ḥ that closes the syllable before -nu is unwritten, as in JA-PA-QA yapqaḥ and JA-RA Yaraḫ. -nu 'our' is the -n(u) 'us' of TA-NA-RA-TE-U-TI-NU.

Weak points: Hebrew has the pattern nîḥōaḥ, and the noun with y is Aramaic and late; 'our' stands beside 'my incense'. Rivals: a personal name; NI 'figs' with JA-NU 'wine' (Ugaritic yn), without a divider or a numeral. One attestation. See QI-TI-RI, DA-KI-TI.
