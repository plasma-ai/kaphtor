### Note

ZA 11b .1–.2 E-TO-RI-\[?\] \*516 / SA-\*301-RI · GRA+PA 1 / I OLIV 1. The word stands in the recipient's place, after the heading and before a measure of grain.

šannāri 'Cat', with \*301 = na: SA-NA-RI, s-n-r, the geminate nn unwritten. Ugarit has the name in both scripts: syllabic sí-na-ru, sí-na-ri, sí-na-a-r\[i\] (Kinlaw 1967, 101) and alphabetic snr, which Watson (1990, 127) finds alternating with Sinarānu for one man.

Sanmartín reads it 'cat' and sets it under one etymon with the Euphrates name Surān (GlOS 2, 201), so both orders of the consonants are Bronze Age: s-n-r at Ugarit, s-r-n to the east, as Akk. šurānu with the personal name Šu-ra-nu (CAD Š/3, 339) and Amorite Šurānatum (Gelb 1980, no. 5952).

The Talmud still knows the two as words of different places: one dreams of a cat 'in a place where they call it shunnara' or 'where it is called shurana' (b. Ber. 56b; Jastrow 1537).

The s-n-r order continues in Judaic Aramaic šunnārā, Syriac ܣܢܘܪܐ sannūrā 'a cat, a wild cat', whose first vowel is the a that SA writes, beside ܫܘܢܪܐ šūnārā 'a cat' (Payne Smith 1903, 382, 566), Arabic sinnawr and sunnār (Lane 1445), and Mehri sənnáwrət.

Animal words serve as names on the tablets, as DU-PA₃-NA Dubbān 'bear'.

Weak point: DULAT marks the Ugaritic name 'etym. unc.' (753). Rival: a name from the town Sināru, Tell Rās Basīṭ (DULAT 752–753; Astour 1995, 58). Rival: ṣinnār 'fast-flowing stream', Ugaritic ṣnr (GlOS 2, 220), which the sign ZI of the syllabic spellings allows. One attestation.
