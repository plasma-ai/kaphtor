### Note

HT 31, the vessel tablet, written with the \*402-VS vessel sign among QA-PA₃, SU-PU and KA-RO-PA₃; Ventris and Chadwick also take it for a vessel. Punic zbr is a vessel counted beside sp in one sanctuary inventory, spm šnm wzbrm šnm 'two sp and two zbr' (KAI 137.6; Tomback 1978, 93; DNWSI s.v. zbr₁), as HT 31 counts SU-PA₃-RA beside SU-PU; Duhoux supposes the two names akin (Duhoux 2002, 40, 45–46). Syriac zābūrā is a small dish or cup (scutella; calix, patina), the libation vessels of the Tabernacle table in Exod 25:29 (Hebrew qəśāwōt), and zābūrtā is also a measure (Payne Smith 1879, col. 1079). The sign is a small handleless cup, nearly conical, 300 of them beside the 10 SU-PU. PA₃ writes ba and RA r, so b and r stand as written. Linear B has a vessel name in -su-\*56, ku-ru-su-\*56 before \*207 (KN K 740), which Melena takes for a Minoan loan (Duhoux 2002, 48). Weak point: SU writes z here, where the other seven words with z use the Z series (RU-ZU-NA); PA₃ = ba is a proposed value, the /ba/ Melena gives Linear B \*56 (Melena 1987, apud Duhoux 2002, 46 n. 26). Rival: Gordon's Heb. sēpel (ספל) 'bowl', √s-p-l, with PA for p and RA for l (Gordon 1966 §115): Ugaritic spl 'platter, tray' (DULAT 754), Old Syrian sà-ap-lu (GlOS 2, 202), Egyptian group writing ṯi₂-pa-ra (GlOS 2, 203, after Hoch, SWET 364, no. 541); with PA₃ = ba it needs p voiced to b. One attestation.
