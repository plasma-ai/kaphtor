### Note

HT 34 .6 SA+MU+KU 100, three signs in ligature spelling the word, under SA-RA₂ after MI+JA+I 245; PA₃ 70 and KI-RO 30 follow and make up the 100. ṣammūqu 'raisins', Peruzzi's comparison (1958, 13): Ug. ṣmqm, issued by the ltḥ beside dried figs (KTU 4.14:17) and by the quart (4.751:10; DULAT 775); Heb. ṣimmūqîm, a hundred in 1 Sam 25:18 and again in 2 Sam 16:1 (HALOT 1033). SA keeps the older a: a Babylonian name is written ṣa-am-mu-uq-qu and Egyptian group writing has ḏa-ma₃-qa in a place name (GlOS 2, 218), where Hebrew has i in the closed syllable (Gordon apud Peruzzi 1958, 13 n. 3). Punic ṣmq is a dried fruit, perhaps the raisin (DNWSI 970); Akk. muzīqu, munziqu 'raisin' has the consonants transposed (CAD M/2 322). Weak point: one document.
