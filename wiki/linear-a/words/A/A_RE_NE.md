### Note

KN Zf 13, signs 1–3, opening the spiral on the gold ring HM 530 (GORILA IV 152–153). ʾAren, the owner's name: Heb. ʾĂrān, an Edomite (Gen 36:28), cuneiform Aranu, 'ibex' beside Akk. armu and Syr. ʾarnā, a comparison HALOT marks with a query (HALOT 89), as ʾArnān (1 Chr 3:21; HALOT 90); Ug. ảrn, a man's name (KTU 4.832:3; DULAT 100). The gold ring PO Zf 2, from a tomb at Poros, bears a name on its bezel (Dimopoulou and Olivier 2022). SI-DI-\*301 Ṣidna follows: 'Aren of Sidon', as lists from Ugarit set a man's name before his city, \]PN URU ṣi-d\[u-na-iu\] (PRU 6 81:4), \]PN ṣd\[ (KTU 4.262:2; DULAT 769). Weak point: E for the ā. Rival: ʾarān 'chest', Ug. ảrn w mznm 'a chest and scales' (KTU 4.385:5; DULAT 100), 'coffin' in first-millennium Phoenician, where Tabnit of Sidon pairs the two words, mlk ṣdnm … škb bʾrn z (KAI 13), and Ahiram's inscription opens with the word, ʾrn z pʿl \[ʾ\]tbʿl (KAI 1:1); Sidon is also a man's name (Gen 10:15; HALOT 1021), which gives 'the coffin of Ṣidon', its owner. Akkadian arānu is Standard and Neo-Babylonian (CAD A/2 231). The Phoenician coffins say 'this coffin', z or zn, and the ring has no demonstrative. The ring lay in compartment E of Chamber Tomb IX at Mavro Spilio, the compartment of a larnax (Olivier 1982). Rival: Aaron, Αρων, Eg. ʿꜣ rn 'the name is great' (HALOT 19). One attestation.
