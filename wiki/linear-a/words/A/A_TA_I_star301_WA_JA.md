### Note

The core libation formula verb. 11 attestations across 5 peak sanctuary sites (Iouktas, Kophinas, Troullos, Palaikastro, Syme). Morphology: A- 1cs prefix, -TA- tG prefix, I epenthetic /i/; \*301-WA-JA = root √n-w-y (\*301 = /na/): /ʾatainawaya/ (di Mino, Ya Diktu). tG-stem (middle-reflexive) of √NWY 'to dwell, settle'—the worshipper actively positions herself at the sanctuary before the deity. Hebrew keeps the tG only in relics such as tirgaltî (Hos 11:3; Hutton & Marzouk 2012), and Moabite has Gt wʾltḥm 'and I fought' (KAI 181:11, 15); the act matches Hebrew wāʾetnappal 'and I prostrated myself' (Deut 9:18), a Hitpaʿel. Same paradigm: A-TA-I-\*301-WA-E (PK Za 11a), JA-TA-I-\*301-U-JA (AP Za 1, 3ms). A-TA-I-\*301-DE-KA and TA-NA-I-\*301-(U-)TI(-NU) share the frame but are √ʿ-n-y 'answer' (di Mino, Ya Diktu). Root noun \*301-WA = nawā 'dwelling' attested at IOZa4 \|\| STEM SEMANTICS: Middle-reflexive, NOT causative. The causative ('I cause the deity to dwell') would require the Š-stem (Shafel/Hiphil), not the tG. The tG/hitpael is the Semitic liturgical stem for what the worshipper does to herself in the divine presence—denominative from nāweh 'abode': 'I make myself a dwelling,' the same pattern as hitnabbēʾ 'make oneself a prophet' from nābîʾ. Closest NWS cognate utterance: Exod 15:2 (Song of the Sea) zeh ʾēlî wə-ʾanwēhû—1cs √n-w-y with deity as object, oldest stratum of Hebrew poetry; Targum Onkelos renders it wə-ʾebnē lēh maqdəšā 'I will build Him a sanctuary,' an ancient witness to the habitation reading (rival sense: 'beautify/praise him,' cf. parallel colon wa-ʾărōməmennhû; HALOT files it under II נוה 'glorify,' and the Peshitta and Palestinian Targumim read 'praise'). Frame parallel: wāʾetnappal lipnê YHWH (Deut 9:18) = 'I prostrated myself before YHWH'—same sentence frame (1cs reflexive verb of self-positioning + 'before the deity'). The prefixed -t- (A-TA-I-, not \*A-I-TA-) follows the West Semitic pattern (Hebrew hitpael, Arabic Form V ta-faʿʿala, Ge'ez tä-qätlä), not the Akkadian Gt infix—the earliest epigraphic witness to the East/West Semitic split in -t- morpheme placement \|\| BIBLICAL ATTESTATION SET: nāweh (נָוֶה) 'comely habitation, pasture'—Exod 15:13 (holy nāweh), Ps 23:2 (green nĕʾōt), Isa 33:20 (Zion as unshaken nāweh), Jer 25:30 (YHWH roars from His holy māʿôn, against His nāweh), Ezek 34:14 (good nāweh on the mountains of Israel). Semantic field consistently pairs divine habitation with pastoral landscape—same conceptual frame as the peak sanctuary formula, where the worshipper settles herself at the mountaintop sanctuary. AMORITE: nawûm 'steppe; encampment (man and beast)' (Huffmon, APNM 237)—the pasture-to-settlement step in second-millennium West Semitic \|\| DIALECT EVIDENCE: Palaikastro A-TA-I-\*301-WA-E weakens JA to E, as in A-SA-SA-RA-ME and A-DI-KI-TE-TE there (di Mino, Ya Diktu). The verb opens the prayer as an act of ritual self-positioning—the worshipper takes up dwelling before the deity. See TA-NA-RA-TE-U-TI-NU (pos 7) for the closing acceptance petition Davis 2026 functional gloss: 'gives' (in Salgarella & Petrakis eds., The Wor(l)ds of Linear A, pp. 43–60)
