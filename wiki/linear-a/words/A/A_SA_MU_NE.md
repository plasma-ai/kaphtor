### Note

Hapax on ZAZb3 (Zakros, LMIB). Skeleton (ʾ)-š-m-n matches Sidonian healing deity Eshmun (Phoenician ʾšmn). Archaic /a/-initial vocalism (ʾAšmūn), which Assyrian ᵈIa-su-mu-nu keeps (SAA 2 5 r. iv 14′; Krahmalkov 2000, 82). The name is šmn 'oil': Ugarit already has the god as šmn Šamnu, the noun as a name (Pardee 2002, 283), the later Eshmun (DULAT 817). Rival etymology: \*ṯamāniy- 'eight', Eshmun as the eighth son of Sadykos (Brown 1995, 36). VIN 32 co-occurs, consistent with libation/offering context. Competing commodity reading 'oil, fat' considered but divine name preferred: Eshmun is well-attested as a Phoenician export deity with Sidonian cult center, and theophoric names are common in Linear A administrative texts. Rival: Woudhuizen (2016, 229–230) reads Akkadian assammû 'a large drinking vessel' (CAD A/2 339–340), the pithos's own name. \|\| PKZa12 A-NE (damaged surface between A and NE; sign loss not established) is possibly A-\[SA-MU\]-NE, a vocative in the libation formula; A-\[KO-A\]-NE (PKZa11) is the likelier restoration. See OLE and \*308.
