### Notes

Administrative list; the relations among most entries are not grammatically expressed. GORILA I 170–171 allows a possible divider after PA₃, so PA₃ may be a separate abbreviation or part of PA₃-DA-KU-SE-NE. If separate, PA₃ before a probable personal name and quantity closely parallels Ugaritic/Phoenician bd /bādi/ or /bīdi/ ‘in the charge of’: Kition no. 84 repeats bd + personal name + a count of workmen. Ugaritic CAT 4.145:9–10 similarly has bd rb hršm ‘in the custody of the chief of the craftsmen’; Akkadian writes ša qāti ‘in the charge of’ with NĪG.ŠU before personal names. PA₃ could thus represent b- ‘in/by’ or a local administrative shorthand for bād(i). The latter is not an established Semitic abbreviation, however, and the standard Linear B coda rule does not license omission of final /d/. Linear B pa-ro + personal name ‘in whose care/in the charge of’ is a functional Aegean parallel. The first DA-KU-SE-NE is damaged in two sign positions in SigLA. NI securely denotes figs: AB30 depicts a fig branch and functions as the Aegean fig ideogram (Montecchi 2024, 178, following Neumann 1957, 157–58). TE-\*301 occurs only on HT8a and HT98a and may spell Semitic /tēnā/ ‘fig,’ but that candidate does not establish NI’s meaning. NI 40 is not marked as a total and no KU-RO occurs. U-TA₂ probably labels an entity or place; Uta/Uda is a comparison, not an identification. Exact Dakušenni is not attested at Nuzi: /Takušenni/ is a cautious reconstruction from Nuzi Aku-šenni, Takku, and Takuja, Boğazköy Tagu(b)-šini, and Ugaritic Taki-šenni; only Hurrian -šenni ‘brother’ is secure as an onomastic comparison. DA-KU-NA matches attested Daguna and is safest here as a personal name, probably Dagan/Dagon-derived. The commodity status of \*188 is secure, while the Pistacia identification remains probable. KI-RA is tentatively compared with Akkadian kirû ‘garden, orchard,’ which fits HT103 and its heading position before a fig entry on ZA8; Akkadian qīru ‘liquid bitumen’ is a formally close but contextually weaker alternative. Neither is established.

## Corpus note

GORILA I 170–171. The tablet is damaged along the left edge, but GORILA marks no lost sign before PA₃. It allows a possible interpunct between PA₃ (AB56) and DA-KU-SE-NE; their segmentation is therefore uncertain. If PA₃ is separate, its position before a probable personal name and quantity closely parallels Ugaritic/Phoenician bd /bādi/ or /bīdi/ ‘in the charge of’: Kition no. 84 repeats bd + personal name + counted workmen (Krahmalkov 2000, 97). PA₃ may accordingly be b- ‘in/by’ or a local shorthand for bād(i), but Semitic spelling does not independently establish bd &gt; b, and Linear B does not normally omit final /d/. The functional Aegean parallel is Linear B pa-ro + personal name ‘in whose care/in the charge of’ (Killen 2024, 103). The first DA-KU-SE-NE is damaged in two sign positions in SigLA. NI 40 is an item, not an expressed total: no KU-RO occurs, and the remaining quantities must not be subtracted from it. NI is the conventional Aegean fig ideogram (AB30/FIC); TE-\*301, if \*301 = NA, is separately comparable with Semitic /tēnā/ ‘fig’ but does not prove NI’s value. U-TA₂ probably labels an entity or place; Uta/Uda is only a comparative name reading. DA-KU-SE-NE is a probable Hurrian-style name in -šenni ‘brother,’ not an exactly attested Dakušenni. KI-RA remains uncertain: Akkadian kirû ‘garden, orchard’ fits its two contexts, while qīru ‘liquid bitumen’ is a formally close alternative.
