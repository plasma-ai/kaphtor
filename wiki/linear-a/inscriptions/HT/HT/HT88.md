### Notes

HT88 is substantially intact. KI-RO introduces exactly six one-unit labels and KU-RO 6 closes their sum; the lower section is therefore a secure outstanding/deficit roster, although not every label is demonstrably a personal name. A-DU is translated contextually as 'work assignment/account(?)' from Akkadian adū/addū; Addu/Haddu remains an alternative. VIR+KA is securely personnel, while kāribū is only a proposed expansion. RE-ZA is (ʾa)raz 'cedar', counted beside cyperus on KH 86; Luwian aruza, a measure, is the rival. KI-KI-NA is Kikkina, supported by Amorite KIKKIN names; the former Ugaritic 'pot' comparison incorrectly omitted final -t. KU-PA₃-NU is a probable personal name or title, compared with Ugaritic gu-pa-na/gpn and Linear B ku-pa-nu-we-to; Kubānu 'devotee/priest of Kubaba' remains an alternative project hypothesis, not an attested title. Payare is supported as a Minoan name by Linear B pa-ja-ro, not translated as Egyptian 'He-of-Re.' Samarō remains a name with NWS and Egyptian comparisons. DA-TA-RE is Daṯar 'Old', Amorite DA-ŠU-RU-UM (Gelb no. 1490), the name of DA-TA-RA on HT6a; rival ḏā-ṯaġri 'He of the gate'.

## Corpus note

The face is substantially intact; minor abrasion does not require restoring a word. KI-RO introduces six one-unit labels and KU-RO 6 closes their exact sum, securely identifying the lower portion as an outstanding/deficit roster. A-DU is here glossed contextually as a possible work assignment/account (Akkadian adū/addū), with Addu/Haddu retained as an alternative. VIR+KA is a personnel class; kāribū remains a lexical hypothesis. RE-ZA is a counted category or measure. NI is figs, followed by Kikkina, a recipient or named entity. KU-PA₃-PA₃ is Kubaba conditional on PA₃=/ba/. KU-PA₃-NU is preferably a personal name or title, compared with Ugaritic gu-pa-na/gpn and Linear B ku-pa-nu-we-to; Kubānu 'devotee/priest of Kubaba' remains an alternative project hypothesis. PA-JA-RE is the Minoan name Payare, supported by Linear B pa-ja-ro. SA-MA-RO is Samarō, with Northwest Semitic and Egyptian comparisons. KA-JU and DA-TA-RE remain unread labels.
