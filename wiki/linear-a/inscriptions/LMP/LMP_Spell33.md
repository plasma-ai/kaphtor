## Translation

**CANDIDATE**, complete.

> Rot! Turn aside! Turn back! Go out, strangling-demon! My Healer, the Great One! Mother!

### Notes

Context: Keftiu medical incantation against samānu disease (mycetoma/ergotism). London Medical Papyrus lines ~11,8–12. Recorded in Egyptian group-writing (syllabic orthography), not Linear A script. Seven foreign words with Egyptian determinatives, and the Egyptian epithet pꜣ wr 'the Great One' between the two divine names. Copied late 18th Dynasty, 14th century BCE (Steiner 1992, n. 5). The heading is Egyptian, 'Incantation of the samānu-disease', and the spell is followed by the instruction 'this spell to be said 4 times.' Cf. Steiner 1992 (JNES 51) who reads spells #27–31 and #33 as NWS but skips #32 as 'non-Semitic.' Davis 2026b (83–84 n. 327) agrees: Steiner's NWS interpretation is 'convincing', and the first theonym reads ra-\[pa/pi\]-ja, rpʾī, with Albright, against Haider's ra-za-ja. He denies #33 to the Keftiu, since its title does not name them. Akulov 2023 provides the most detailed modern transliteration; Akulov reads through Hattic, we read through NWS.

EXTERNAL SOURCE: Not Linear A script. Keftiu language recorded in Egyptian group-writing (syllabic orthography) in the London Medical Papyrus (BM 10059), 14th century BCE. STRUCTURE: Disease addressed + two imperatives (turn aside, turn back) + 'go out' + demon name + deity invocation + Egyptian epithet 'the Great One' + deity invocation. Classic ANE incantation formula: (1) command disease to depart, (2) name the demon (= power over it), (3) invoke protective deities. STEINER 1992 CONTEXT: Steiner (JNES 51) reads all surrounding spells (#27–31, #33) as NWS (Phoenician-Aramaic mix) but explicitly skips #32 as 'non-Semitic.' DEITY PAIRING: rpʾī (Healer), the Great One, + ʾimmā (Mother), as Spell 27 pairs 'our Lord' with Ištar-ummī 'my mother Ištar' (Steiner 1992, 193). Sources: Akulov 2017/2023 (transliteration, Hattic reading), Steiner 1992 (NWS partial reading), Kyriakidis 2002 (phonological analysis), Wreszinski 1912 (editio princeps), Haider 2001 (initial identification of Keftiu deities).
