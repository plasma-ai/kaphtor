## Translation

**CANDIDATE**, complete.

### Notes

Context: Keftiu medical incantation against samānu disease (mycetoma/ergotism). London Medical Papyrus lines ~11,8–12. Recorded in Egyptian group-writing (syllabic orthography), not Linear A script. Six Keftiu words (five written elements, one containing two words) with Egyptian determinatives marking word boundaries and grammatical classes. Copied late 18th Dynasty, 14th century BCE (Steiner 1992, n. 5). The spell is preceded by the Egyptian frame: 'A spell about samānu illness \[that\] spreads through grain and soil:' and followed by the instruction 'this spell to be said 4 times.' Cf. Steiner 1992 (JNES 51) who reads spells #27–31 and #33 as NWS but skips #32 as 'non-Semitic.' Akulov 2023 provides the most detailed modern transliteration; Akulov reads through Hattic, we read through NWS.  EXTERNAL SOURCE: Not Linear A script. Keftiu language recorded in Egyptian group-writing (syllabic orthography) in the London Medical Papyrus (BM 10059), 14th century BCE. STRUCTURE: Double imperative (disease expulsion) + vocative demon name + deity invocation + invocation verb + deity invocation. Classic ANE incantation formula: (1) command disease to depart, (2) name the demon (= power over it), (3) invoke protective deities. STEINER 1992 CONTEXT: Steiner (JNES 51) reads all surrounding spells (#27–31, #33) as NWS (Phoenician-Aramaic mix) but explicitly skips #32 as 'non-Semitic.' DEITY PAIRING: rpʾī (Healer) + Mayyā (Water) = cure + purification. The Ugaritic Rapʾu cult (KTU 1.108) and the water-based ritual instructions ('recite over liquid') confirm both elements. Sources: Akulov 2017/2023 (transliteration, Hattic reading), Steiner 1992 (NWS partial reading), Kyriakidis 2002 (phonological analysis), Wreszinski 1912 (editio princeps), Haider 2001 (initial identification of Keftiu deities).
