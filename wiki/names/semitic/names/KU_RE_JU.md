## Note

The HT 117b roster, headed \*21F-TU-NE, fixes the personal-name slot, KU-RE-JU 1 before DI-KI-SE 1; on HT 39 KU-RE-JU takes 8. With R/L neutralization and JU = lu, the spelling writes ku-le-lu.

Kulēlu is the diminutive of KU-RO kull: \*kulaylu, 'little perfect one', with ay &gt; ē. One root carries 'all', 'to complete, make perfect' and 'to surround, to crown', and a name built on it calls its bearer whole or perfect. Ugaritic writes the same pattern the same way, ku-re-ku /kurēk/, {qutēl} &lt; \*qutayl, the pattern that forms diminutives across Semitic (Tropper 2000, 274), and Arabic builds personal names on it, Ḥumayd, Quraysh (Fischer 2002 §81 n. 3). RE fixes the vowel: kull itself would be KU-RO, the wreath kilīlu KI-RI-JU, the adjective kullulu KU-RU-JU.

The root makes names in each branch. Hebrew has the bare noun, Kᵉlāl 'perfection', a man of the Return (Ezra 10:30). Akkadian names a child for the wreath, kilīlu 'circlet, headband': Ilum-kilīlī 'God is my wreath' (Ur III), Irra-kilīlī (Old Babylonian), Nanā-kilīlī-uṣri (CAD K 358), and the one-word name {m}ki-li-lu at Middle Assyrian Dunni-Aššur (Tell Sabi Abyad), T 98-038 obv. 4' (ORACC tcma/tsa1, P532066, normalized Kulilu there). The D adjective kullulu 'crowned, veiled' (CAD K 518) is the Neo-Assyrian name Kullulu, {1}ku-lu-lu, SAA 6 22 rev. 9'. Amorite has KU-LA-IL-E (Gelb 1980, no. 4114) and the names in -kulla, be-el-ku-ul-la (GlOS 1, 305).

The quantity 8 on HT 39 and DI-KI-SE, a name in the same layout, show a name. Rival: the animal name, Ebla gú-li-lu kulīlu 'dragonfly' (Pagan 1998) and Mār-Kulilum at Old Babylonian Sippar (CT 8 34a:2; de Boer 2014, 358), which Linear A would write KU-RI-JU. Weak point: HT 117a writes the diphthong open in KO-SA-I-TI, where KU-RE-JU contracts it. Every reading takes JU = lu.

## As a name

The HT117b roster fixes the personal-name slot. The Middle Assyrian Kulilu parallel is formally close but does not establish the Linear A name's etymology; Amorite k-l-l names remain secondary comparisons. Kulilu is written ki-li-lu at Tell Sabi Abyad.
