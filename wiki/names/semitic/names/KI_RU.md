## Note

MI Zb 1, incised on the base of a clay vase (Copenhagen NM 6960): KI-RU, two signs, nothing else (GORILA IV 91). An owner's name: Ug. krr, syllabic ki-ri-ri (DULAT 452); KE-KI-RU Kikkaru (HT 94b) ends in the same signs. Names are the commonest single word on a vase, as A-PA₃-DI (PK Zb 26), DI-NA-U (KN Zb 27) and A-RE-SA-NA (THE Zb 2). Weak point: ki-ri-ri has three syllables to KI-RU's two. Rival: Akk. kirru A, a large vessel of earthenware, metal or stone for water, beer, oil, wine, honey or perfume, from Old Akkadian on (CAD K 408–410), the geminate unmarked; Akkadian alone attests it, the reading of DUG.KIR (CAD K 409), and no other vase is labelled with the word for a vase. Rival: Heb. kiyyôr 'basin, laver' (Exod 30:18), Akk. kiūru 'metal cauldron', a Neo-Assyrian foreign word (CAD K 476), which needs an unwritten y. One attestation.

## As a name

MI Zb 1, the one word incised on the base of a clay vase from Melos (GORILA IV 91). Kirru: Ugaritic krr, a man's name, ki-ri-ri (DULAT 452). Weak point: three syllables there against two here. Rivals: Akkadian kirru 'large vessel', Hebrew kiyyôr 'basin'. See the lexicon entry.
