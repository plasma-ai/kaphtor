## Note

KO Za 1 .2 TU-RU-SA · DU-\*314-RE · I-DA-A ·. TU-RU-SA follows the formula verb A-TA-I-\*301-WA-JA, the place of the one invoked, where Iouktas has JA-DI-KI-TU and Troullos O-SU-QA-RE. His title follows him, ḏū har ʾIda 'the One of Mount Ida', and its ḏū is masculine, so the one invoked at Kophinas is a god.

Tursa 'Shield' is the base \*turs- 'shield, door-bar': Arabic turs 'a shield', tatarrasa 'he defended himself with a shield' (Lane 302), Jewish Aramaic tᵉrîsā and post-biblical Hebrew tᵉrîs 'shield' (Jastrow 1698). The Targum writes tᵉrîsā for māgēn where the god is the shield (Ps 3:4), and Hebrew calls its god a shield from Abraham on (Gen 15:1; Ps 84:12; HALOT 545). TU-RU writes the cluster rs with its echo vowel.

A people and a man bear the name. The Tursha are a Sea People in the Egyptian records, whom HALOT sets beside Hebrew Tîrās (Gen 10:2) and Greek Τυρσηνοί (HALOT 1728–1729); Punic has the man's name TRŠ, 'Turesh, the Etruscan?' (Krahmalkov 2000, 499); Mari has Tursa as a place or ethnic name (FM V, 281). The shield-bearing Kouretes of Ida guard the infant god, taking their name from the Tursha (di Mino, Ya Diktu).

One attestation. See DU-\*314-RE, I-DA-A.
