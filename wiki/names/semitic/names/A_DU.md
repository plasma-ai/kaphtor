## Note

HT 92 TE · A-DU · GRA 680 · \*304 12 and HT 133 A-DU · TE · GRA+DA 55: TE terûmā 'contribution, offering' with grain booked directly to A-DU, the god as recipient. The 680 of HT 92 may total the barley collected for A-DU (Montecchi 2008, 320). Gordon's Addu/Haddu (Gordon 1966 §129): the spelling without h fits Old Babylonian Amorite and Akkadian Addu, written second in LA-AḪ-WI-A-DU (Gelb 1980, no. 4216), and second-millennium syllabic forms such as eḫ-li-a-du keep the divine element (Howard 2023, 5); Ugaritic has ảdd beside hd and hdd, ỉl hd 'the god Haddu' (KTU 1.10 II 5), ỉl ảdd (KTU 1.65:9; DULAT 329); Ebla has ʾa₃-du as a personal name (Pagan 1998, row 27). The god stands first in A-DU-NI-TA-NA, A-DU-ZA and A-DU-KU-MI-NA, and second in KO-A-DU-WA.

Alalakh kept god and ration apart by the determinative, ᵈIŠKUR beside a-du (RIME 4.34.1.1:2; Draffkorn 1959, 152–54); the homograph A-DU adû heads the personnel and ration lists. Ugaritic ritual texts book the storm god's offerings to bʿl, bʿl š 'Baʿlu: a ram' (KTU 1.39:6; Pardee 2000, 970), as HT 116a books PU-RA₂ GRA 40 among its recipients. Rival: adû, 'rations: contribution, grain 680'. Weak point: SigLA queries the first sign of HT 92 as a transaction sign, and neither tablet carries another word. See A-DU, TE and PU-RA₂.

## As a name

The god Addu as recipient of the grain booked with TE on HT 92 and HT 133 (Gordon 1966 §129): see lexicon A-DU (HT92, HT133). On HT 85a, 86a, 88, 95b and 99a the same spelling heads personnel and ration lists as the word adû 'work assignment, ration' (lexicon A-DU); KH 23 A-DU\[ breaks after DU and stays unread.
