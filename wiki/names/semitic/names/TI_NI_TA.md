## Note

HT 27a opens TI-NI-TA · \*307, the goddess with her sanctuary, over a roster of 335 men, Il-Mīšar heading 43 or more. The variant TA-NA-TI is commoner (HT 7a, 10b, 49a, 98a).

Punic in Greek letters writes Θινιθ on El-Hofra EH 1 (Krahmalkov 2000, 126), beside Θεννειθ (ibid., 495), and Neo-Punic adds a vowel letter, so Tinnit, the vowels of TI-NI-TA (Hvidberg-Hansen 1979, I 47). Her home is the east. Megiddo yields her sign in the 11th century BCE, the earliest known (Arie 2017, 61–62), two of Tyre's funerary stelae carry names with tnt (ibid., 65), Carthage dedicates to 'Tinnit in Lebanon' (KAI 81.1), and ʿbdtnt 'servant of Tinnit' is a Phoenician name (Benz 1972, 164).

At Kommos, Phoenicians built a three-pillar shrine over the Minoan buildings (Shaw 1989, 180–181), and Shaw counts Tanit among the deities its pillars may stand for (Muñoz Sogas 2022, 26). One attestation. See TI-NI-TA \*307 and TA-NA-TI.
