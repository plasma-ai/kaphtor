## Note

HT 115a.2, erased, with no number after it; the tablet is a palimpsest that lists persons with grain and cattle (GORILA I 188–91).

Nawra 'Bright': na-u-ra, \*301 writing na and U the w of the diphthong, as KA-U-DE-TA and A-RA-U-DA write a-u across two signs. It is the adjective of √n-w-r: Akkadian namru, older nawru, 'bright, shiny, radiant' (CAD N/1, 239), a personal name in Old Babylonian as Na-aw-ru-um, Na-aw-ru-um-ì-lí and feminine Na-wi-ir-tum, in Middle Assyrian as Nam-ru (CAD N/1, 243). Ebla has the name na-wa-ru₁₂ (ARET 20 24), Tuttul ʾabī-nawar and šumu-nawar (Buck 2018, 321, 349). Gelb lists Amorite nawarum under NWR 'to shine', with the feminines nīra and nūra in -a (Gelb 1980, glossary). The heading name of the same tablet, \*47-NU-RA-JA, spells the noun nūr 'light' NU-RA, so the scribe keeps adjective and noun apart.

The root: Ug. n-r 'to shine' and nr 'sheen, gleam; lamp' (DULAT 633), /nāra/, /yanūr-/ (Huehnergard 2012, 50); Heb. nēr 'lamp' and the name Nēr (HALOT 723); Ar. nūr. 'Light' is a common name element: Amorite Niwru and ʾAbu-niwra (Gelb 1980, nos. 5026, 162), Ebla nu-ru₁₂-ud 'light' (Pagan 1998, row 3919), Ugaritic nrn and nryn.

Weak point: one witness, erased. Rival: naʿar 'servant, retainer' (Heb. נַעַר), which leaves the U unexplained. Rival: \*301 = r, which gives r-u-ra, an awkward reduplication. \*301-U recurs in JA-TA-I-\*301-U-JA and TA-NA-I-\*301-U-TI-NU. See NU-RO, \*47-NU-RA-JA.

## As a name

Reading from the lexicon (di Mino). In erasure on HT 115a.2 (GORILA I, 188–91). Rival: naʿar 'servant, retainer' (√n-ʿ-r; Heb. נַעַר), which suits a list of people and grain just as well.
