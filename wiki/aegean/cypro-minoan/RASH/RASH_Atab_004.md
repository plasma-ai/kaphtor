## Translation

ʾAġlā, son of Mali. ʾAdī, son of ʿAmmusapi. ʾIšbaʿl. ʾAneni, the Maʿqabian. ʾAdattari, son of Tayyānu. ʾIyapati, son of \[…\]. Son of ʾEwenni. ʾAwesapi, son of Kabili. Zidanni. ʾIlyānu, the Siyannian. Danānu, son of ʾIlī-ṯtamar. ʾAnniyānu, son of Sanuwani. ʾUl-ʾAmurru, the Gapanian. Kôṯar-ʾabī. ʾIlī-malik, son of ʾUmmī-môtu. ʾAġalʾabī, son of the king. ʾEwri-tašal, ʾarabimasakuli. ʿAmmurapi; minister of the king \[…\]. Mosani, son of the king. Šapši-malik, scribe; pa.

di Mino's reading, CANDIDATE, incomplete; 50 words.

A list of men, each named alone, with his father ('PN son of PN') or with his town ('PN, of GN'), as É. Masson read it (1974, 38) after Schaeffer's suggestion of a list of names (O. Masson 1969, 392 n. 22), closing with lines that name a minister and a scribe (B.17–19; É. Masson 1974, 34–35; Valério 2016, 356). Its names are those of Ugarit, Semitic with a Hurrian share, and its bn 'son' and its names in the genitive, ʾIlyāni of Siyannu among them, point to a Semitic language, as Valério concludes (2016, 395). Most identifications are those of Saporetti, Nahm, Faucounau and Valério, credited word by word, read here on one value for each sign.

Of 300 random groups of the same length drawn from the tablet's own signs and set against the syllabic names of the Akkadian texts of Ugarit (Kinlaw 1967), none fits as closely as Kôṯar-ʾabī or ʿAmmurapi, two as closely as ʾIlī-malik and six as ʾIlyānu; the value of 074 was chosen on ʿAmmurapi. Two Enkomi tablets, ENKO Atab 003 and 004, read on the same values, fit the same names in 10% and 3% of their legible groups of three signs or more, against 26% here. One legible group, in A.06, stays unread.

The tablet tells nothing of the languages Cypro-Minoan wrote on Cyprus (É. Masson 1974, 47).
