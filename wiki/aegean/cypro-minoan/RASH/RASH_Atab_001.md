Small boustrophedon clay tablet (RS 17.06, ##212) excavated in 1953 in the private library of Rašap-abu at Ugarit (É. Masson 1974, 24–30; Valério 2016, 399, 540–543). Likely a letter: opening address with two consecutive -023 (-ti) words wrapped across boustrophedon line ends (A.01–02 and A.02–03), followed on A.03 by a group where the scribe erased a mistakenly carried-over -ti, and a two-word left-edge signature (B.lat.sin.).

Unlike RASH Atab 004 (RS 20.25), this tablet has no 051-028 (binu), yields zero decoy-calibrated Kinlaw 1967 or Monti 2017 matches at p &lt;= 0.05 on the ruled grid, and shares two exact lexical parallels with Cyprus: 019-023 (u-ti) with ENKO Arou 001 (CM 1) and 006-025 (pa-ka) with ENKO Atab 002 (CM 2). The productive -o-ti / -ti ending points to an indigenous Cypriot / pre-Eteocypriot matrix grammar beside local Ugarit or Hurrian personal names (such as ka-se-ji), against Faucounau's Hurrian reading (1977, 235–236).
