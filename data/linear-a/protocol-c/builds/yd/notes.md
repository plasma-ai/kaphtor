# Transcription notes

The inventory was checked against all 59 rows of the first table in units.md. Row numbers below count its data rows from 1, excluding the header and separator. Every named section or paragraph was read in full, including continuations. The supplied text is byte-for-byte identical to a fresh layout-preserving extraction of the 46-page PDF. Pages 5, 13, 43 and 44 were also inspected visually. Printed and PDF page numbers agree.

## Conflicting requirements and source labels

The final output prohibition conflicts with the request for an exact full bibliographic title and exact appendix paragraph labels. The citation therefore retains the author, short title and date; its subtitle is withheld. The appendix source field uses “Appendix B”, which occurs verbatim in the section column of units.md. The individual paragraph labels contain prohibited whole-word material and are not reproduced. The row ledger below preserves their locations and coverage without copying that material. The other unit labels are copied verbatim. Repeated labels are listed once in units_read.

The requested bound-affix spellings are retained as morphology, including their original capitals and boundaries. No whole-word host examples or standalone sign-value catalogue has been added. An absolutely literal prohibition on every character sequence that can also serve as a sign reading would conflict with the request to transcribe affixes themselves. This cannot be fully reconciled within the brief.

## Segmentation and classification limits

- Entries yd_031, yd_032, yd_034 and yd_035 have no independently supplied phonemic reading in their appendix paragraphs. Their consonantal cover symbols retain the S-series merger and unresolved stop distinction described in §9, p. 21: S is an unresolved sibilant, T an unresolved dental-series stop reading. Each is one segment and is not expanded into guessed alternatives. These are conservative spellings, not established phonemic analyses. The grammar describes series, rather than explicitly prescribing this inventory's segment notation; this application of the cover-symbol rule is an interpretive limitation.
- In those four entries the short vowels represent the written vowel letters. Whether each vowel is pronounced, silent, or conceals length is unresolved. The schema has no unknown-segmentation field and requires nonempty segments. Thus the inventory retains the printed forms with visibly unresolved segmentations rather than silently dropping them. These entries do not establish fully phonemic inventories.
- The reading of the adjectival ending in row 38 is especially uncertain. The grammar also offers a free-clause interpretation. Its sounds have not been imported into the suffix analysis. The bound adjectival analysis itself is explicitly printed and therefore enters.
- The gender or case function of the roster ending (yd_032) and the inflectional component in row 49 (yd_033) is not stated. Their assignment to s_case_person is a provisional use of the nominal inflection slot, not an identification of a particular case. A fully grammar-determined slot cannot be supplied for either.
- The “from/of” suffix is placed with nominal relational inflection, and the explicitly terminative suffix with case endings. The author does not explicitly call either a clitic or a free postposition.
- The gentilic ending (yd_030), prosthetic vowel (yd_029), and inflectional component (yd_033) use readings available for their spellings elsewhere in the listed tables. Their own paragraphs do not independently specify phonemic length or every orthographic detail. No hidden consonant or long vowel has been added.
- The verbal spelling in yd_017b is explicitly a t-based second-person feminine marker on p. 45. Its two-phoneme analysis follows the corresponding subject marker in the p. 13 table. The p. 45 passage alone does not establish its vowel length. It also reports uncertainty in the last sign and permits an alternative reading in which the prefix alone marks person. No additional suffix or zero realization has been manufactured for that alternative.
- The determinative in yd_024 is written inside the preceding host and is therefore placed in s_enclitic. The author names it a determinative and describes its position before the following noun, but does not explicitly call it an enclitic. Its bound status and suffix placement remain a classification judgment.
- The epenthetic vowel and prosthetic vowel enter because they are expressly named formative material in the listed units. Neither is assigned an invented inflectional function; p_stem is the closest available slot.
- The longer tG opening in yd_002d is hyphen-delimited in the p. 19 reading and explicitly listed as a prefix in units.md. The grammar calls the full formation tG, but does not separately parse this opening into person and derivational components. It shares the tG morpheme identifier with the shorter stem-marker notations; that grouping is not proof that all these shapes are freely interchangeable.
- The feminine endings represented by yd_004, yd_022, yd_025 and yd_028 are kept as separate morphemes unless their identity is explicitly stated. No unattested allomorph relationship is imposed. The two spellings under yd_022 are expressly equated on p. 46.

## Optional segments, zero forms and order

The table on p. 13 prints /n(u)/. Entries yd_018 and yd_018b preserve that identical printed form and respectively omit or include its parenthesized vowel. No other optional segment is supplied for a target affix. Parenthesized material in comparison examples is not an additional target realization.

No explicit zero-ending symbol is printed as an affix in the listed target analyses. §5.3, p. 13, explicitly says the noun is “without an overt case ending”; no ending is entered for that absence. §5.2, p. 11, discusses construct-state vowel reduction but supplies no separable construct-state affix. This is recorded as an unrepresented state realization, not converted into an invented suffix.

The default prefix order is retained. On the suffix side, s_mood precedes s_case_person because §6.1, pp. 13–14, explicitly puts the indicative before the subject pronoun. The finite subject marker belongs to s_case_person; the final object belongs to s_pronoun. The inner-enclitic slot is unused. The conjunction and presentative, and the two outer enclitics of the expanded form, occupy the same respective classes. The schema cannot express their internal order within one slot; the entry notes retain the stated order.

## Disagreements, exclusions and repeated statements

The p. 8 table identifies the initial material of the adverbial nominal as part of its root, and its prose rejects an earlier nominalizing-prefix analysis. The p. 42 appendix nevertheless calls the initial alternation a “prefix variant”. Entries yd_007 through yd_007d preserve that explicitly named variant under the nominal-pattern prefix slot, with the contradiction unresolved. They must not be taken as proof of a productive nominalizing prefix.

The table and prose on p. 7 give a short-vowel vocative, while the reading and appendix give the long-vowel spelling. Both are retained. The initial alternative on p. 41 is also explicitly named vocative. The possible vocative in §8 is tentative; its comparative glottal onset has not been imported.

The presentative is printed both without and with an internal hyphen. The possible contracted conjunction-plus-presentative readings are whole combinations, not independently delimited allomorphs of the presentative. No new nasal-plus-vowel suffix or prefix is inferred from those readings.

The nominal mimation statements in Appendix B, pp. 42, 44 and 46, name a category but do not isolate a new ending's shape. The accusative-with-mimation ending already isolated in §4.3 is retained as a single fused ending. No bare nasal ending is reconstructed from the mimation-only statements.

The bound personal suffix translated “my” in §8, p. 18, is consistent with the explicitly printed possessive suffix elsewhere. The appendix's “my abode” analysis on pp. 41–42 does not delimit the claimed first-person suffix from its nominal base. No new ending is cut from that whole word.

The relative accompanying the second-person suffix is not expressly called bound; only the explicitly suffixed pronoun enters. Roots, stem-final vowels, unsegmented independent names, demonstratives and free-clause alternatives do not enter. Comparative affixes, alternate interpretations ascribed to other analyses, and independent quoted examples are not additional target-language morphology.

The tG discussion on p. 5, n. 9, gives alternative ways to interpret the hiatus. It does not give separately isolated affixes with the intervening consonants. None has been constructed. The fully vocalized derived-stem template on p. 26 is likewise not entered as an affix.

The diagnostic afformatives in §9 are composite spellings discussed in an argument about word structure. The author does not assign new minimal affix functions to them there. Generic “case endings” supply no printable form. The appendix alternative attached to the gentilic analysis is not adopted as a second target ending.

No target bound affix is explicitly dependent on a contested reading of its own signs. All contested_value fields are false. Contested material in a host's root does not transfer to its affixes. This does not establish certainty of the unresolved readings above.
