# Transcription notes

All 24 listed units were read, including the seven derived-stem items, the full preposition list, and the two specified 2005 summary items. Attached notes were read as continuations of their units; entries taken from them cite their actual printed pages and note numbers. Every unit contributed entries or repeated realizations already recorded, so `units_without_entries` is empty. Repeated attestations are recorded in entry notes rather than duplicated merely because a table is also inside a listed section. The source-unit labels reproduce the title/caption column of `units.md` verbatim.

## Printed forms and extraction

- The PDFs govern. The 2019 tables do not all use the same reconstruction conventions: Tables 3.5–3.7 omit asterisks on their forms, while Tables 3.8–3.9 print them. These differences have been retained.
- A paradigm often prints a complete inflected word rather than a separate affix. In such cases `form` is the literal affix substring, including its printed adjacent boundary signs, and the entry note supplies the complete source token. No hyphen or asterisk has been added to make an extracted fragment look like a separately printed affix. Thus a suffix extracted from an unhyphenated paradigm cell can lack a hyphen. Root material is not inventoried. This is an interpretive difficulty in applying “exactly as printed” to an inventory of affixes from whole-word paradigms.
- Distinct printed spellings, including differences in boundary notation and reconstruction asterisks, can have identical segments. They share a `morpheme_id` where they represent the same grammatical morpheme. Homophonous morphemes with different functions remain separate. Entry count is consequently not a count of distinct phoneme strings.
- Slash alternatives are separated at the slash: Table 3.5 `-iː/-ja` gives `-iː` and `-ja`; §3.7 `*pa/ʔap` gives `*pa` and `ʔap`. A reconstruction asterisk has not been silently supplied to the second member. The paired dual forms in Table 3.5 are alternatives for common gender, not separate masculine and feminine dual paradigms.
- Optional parentheses remain in `form`. Both absent and present realizations are entered for the four optional plural pronouns, the two feminine-dual relative forms, locative `*-u(m)`, directional `*-ah(a)`, causative `*s(a)`, and the optional prepositional endings. The `±` in Table 3.5 is notation, not a segment. In Table 3.6, optional `aw` is omitted or included together.
- The two energics in the 2011 text are printed separately as `yaqtulan` and `yaqtulanna`. Their endings are transcribed separately; the inventory does not substitute the parenthesized shorthand used in the unit summary.

## Reading and segmentation checks

- Table 3.5, p. 54, prints `-kuma:` with an ordinary colon in the PDF text layer; the matching third-person form is `-sumaː`. The page image confirms length notation in both. The transcription preserves `a:` in the former instead of normalizing it to `aː`. Similarly, §3.3.2.2, p. 59, prints `*maliʔ-u:` and `*maliʔ-a:`. Their suffix segments retain `u:` and `a:`. This is a typographical distinction, not a claim of distinct vowel qualities.
- On p. 58, §3.3.2.1 prints the less common feminine marker `*aːʔ` without an initial hyphen; the OCR supplies one. The PDF spelling is retained.
- The 2019 inflection prose on p. 63 uses `y` in some examples while the paradigm and other prose use `j`. Both printed notations are retained. They have not been silently harmonized.
- Long vowels are single segments, retaining the source's length notation, including `ū` in the innovation sources. Diphthongal sequences are split into their printed vowel and glide. The ejective `t’` is one segment, and `ʦ` is one segment. Written doubled consonants are represented by two identical consonant strings, without inventing a length symbol absent from the printed form.
- `V` in `*jV-`, `*ʔin-tV`, and `*min(V)` is a vowel cover symbol, retained as a single segment. No particular vowel is substituted: these occurrences do not consistently supply a specific two-vowel expansion. The optional realization of `*min(V)` includes one `V` segment. Root-pattern placeholders such as indexed `C` are not treated as phonemes or entered as affixes.
- The 2005 scan, p. 165 n. 23, has plural `*yaqtulūna`; the OCR's `*yaqtulāna` is erroneous. The scan's `ū` is used. The note explicitly identifies `*-na`, allowing the plural ending to be represented as plural `ū` followed by imperfective `na`, rather than treating the whole sequence as an indivisible suffix.
- The syllabic spelling `ʔa₅-na` in the preposition list is not segmented into invented phonemes. The source supplies `/hana/`, which is inventoried with its printed slashes and the segments `h, a, n, a`. Slashes here delimit transcription, not alternatives. The parenthetical multiword example `in(a) balu` is not entered as one bound form; its internal analysis is not supplied in the unit. Explicitly glossed single-word prepositions and the joined form `ʔǝn-zä` are included.
- No selected form remains illegible after the PDF checks. Some analyses and cover-symbol values remain uncertain as described here and in individual entry notes.

## Scope and category difficulties

- The grammar states on p. 66, “Since prepositions are bound forms,” and on p. 67 distinguishes those ending in `=` as “usually proclitic.” I applied the brief's explicit bound-form inclusion rule to the full list, including forms without `=`. This differs from the unit summary's characterization of some prepositions as free. Their entry notes preserve the distinction; no equals signs have been added. Explicitly printed attested prepositional variants are retained without constructing additional variants.
- Table 3.6 is explicitly selected and its relative marker is described as a bound form. The schema has no slot for a bound relative pronoun. It is retained under category `pronominal`, with the whole declined form provisionally placed in `p_clitic` as a bound head preceding its dependent. This is a schema accommodation, not a claim that the table labels the marker a conjunction or particle. The choice of this slot remains an analysis limitation.
- Predicative verbal-adjective subject endings are placed in `s_case_person`, because they mark person, number and gender; they do not form the adjective stem. The `s_stem` description's mention of “verbal-adjective suffixes” makes this distinction imperfectly specified. The functions explicitly say when the source describes adjective predication and when it describes the active perfective. No unattested first-person singular or second-person plural predicative endings have been supplied from the excluded independent-pronoun table.
- The locative and directional endings are placed in `s_case_person` because they are presented in the case-and-declension unit, while their functions preserve the author's preferred description as adverbial endings. The optional preposition extension `*-aj` is placed in `s_stem`; the unit does not assign it a case meaning.
- The short-prefix and imperative paradigms share their printed endings. Their entry functions and secondary citations identify both uses. The alternative person-prefix reconstruction in the note to §3.5.3 and the possible `t-` third-feminine-plural prefix in the note to Table 3.8 are retained as explicitly uncertain proposals, without selecting a preferred reconstruction. Their citations are p. 73 nn. 17–19, where the attached notes actually appear. The contracted causative prefix in the note to §3.5.5 is likewise cited to p. 74 n. 24.
- D-stem doubling, the L-stem vowel after the first radical, R-stem reduplication, root-internal placement of infixed `t`, and internal passive vowel melodies do not supply independent word-edge affixes. They have not been converted into invented concatenative endings. The printed edge prefixes, prefixed `t`, and the explicitly printed `s-t` sequence are retained. Likewise, the basic participle and infinitive patterns do not provide edge affixes; the derived participle prefix `*mu-` does.
- Free independent, demonstrative and interrogative pronouns are excluded. In §3.10, the presentatives `*han` and `*hal` and hypothetical particle `*law` are free and excluded. The existential bases `*jiθ-` and `*ʔajn-` are not expressly called bound, proclitic or enclitic in that unit, nor shown attached to a host. Their final hyphens alone do not establish clitic status, so they are not entered. In §3.7, `*ʔaw` is not included in the statement that the preceding two conjunctions are proclitic, and is excluded.
- The explicitly named enclitic `*-tiː` in §3.1.1 is included even though its hosts are free pronouns; its function is oblique marking. The independent host words themselves are excluded. Similarly, the optional plural extensions named in that section are recorded, but no unprinted complete suffix-pronoun shapes with `na` are constructed.
- The innovation sources contribute only their stated innovations. Historical alternatives and rejected or non-diagnostic features discussed in their prose do not create additional entries. The selected 2019 derived-stem section includes explicitly printed later developments; these are identified as such where relevant rather than promoted to inherited reconstructions.

## Pages, order and zero marking

The 2005 `1. *yaqtulu` heading begins on printed p. 164 and continues on p. 165; the relevant endings and the attached note are on p. 165. Its summary item is on p. 191. `10. Barth-Ginsberg Law` occupies printed pp. 180–181, not pp. 181–182 as in the unit row; p. 182 begins the next section. The selected summary calls this item (11), although the body heading numbers it 10. These are retained as source numbering/page discrepancies, not silently repaired in `units.md`.

The 2019 number prose separates the feminine-plural element and feminine marker in `-aː-t-`; Table 3.7 prints `-aːt-`. Both presentations are recorded, without erasing the printed boundary difference. Table 3.7 explicitly separates case/number-case endings from nonbound-state markers; unsegmented examples elsewhere do not justify additional indivisible `um` or `uːna` morphemes. No further disagreement between the selected paradigm tables was found.

The A2 template order is used unchanged, with an empty `class_order.basis`. The grammar's case-before-state statement and the `s`-before-`t` statement concern order within one template slot, not a different order among slots. The schema does not express all such internal sequencing constraints; assigning case and state to their required common slot should not be read as a claim that they cannot co-occur.

No zero ending is entered. Relevant absences include the generally unmarked masculine singular and some unmarked feminine nouns (§3.3.2.1), absence of nonbound-state `-m`/`-na` on construct forms (§3.3.2.3 and Table 3.7), no overt suffix in the short-prefix 1csg, 1cpl, 2msg and 3msg/3fsg cells, and no overt suffix in the masculine singular imperative. The blank first-person dual cells are not filled by reconstruction. The dash for the first-person dual pronoun is absence of a reconstructed paradigm member, not a phonological zero morpheme. No definite or indefinite article is reconstructed in the selected 2019 unit, and no article form is invented from its historical discussion.
