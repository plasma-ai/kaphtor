A Semitic root is a skeleton of consonants, most often three, holding a core meaning that vowels and affixes shape
into words. The Kaphtor Semitic Roots give a page to each of {roots} roots reconstructed for Proto-Semitic, the
family's common ancestor, filed by first consonant. Each page sets the root and its meaning above the words descended
from it, language by language: {reflexes} forms in {languages} languages, led by Arabic ({arabic}), Hebrew ({hebrew}),
Akkadian ({akkadian}), Syriac ({syriac}), Geʿez ({geez}) and Ugaritic ({ugaritic}), every form cited to the page that
prints it, {citations} citations to {works} works. {sed} rest on Militarev and Kogan's Semitic Etymological Dictionary
and its online continuation, and the other {project} were built for kaphtor from the dictionaries the Kaphtor
Dictionary Distillation records. They are the comparanda of the Linear A readings: {with_root} words of the lexicon
